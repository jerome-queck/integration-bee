## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)\sin(bx)}{x}\,dx,\qquad a>b>0.
\]

## Solution

The product identity \(\sin A\sin B=\tfrac12\bigl(\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)\bigr)\) rewrites the integral as
\[
\frac12\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos((a-b)x)-\cos((a+b)x)}{x}\,dx.
\]
For \(q>p>0\),
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(px)-\cos(qx)}{x}\,dx=\int_{p}^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\varepsilon x}\sin(sx)\,dx\,ds\Big|_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}.
\]
The inner Laplace transform is \(s/(\varepsilon^{2}+s^{2})\), and the \(s\)-integral is \(\tfrac12\ln\bigl((\varepsilon^{2}+q^{2})/(\varepsilon^{2}+p^{2})\bigr)\). Sending \(\varepsilon\downarrow 0\) produces \(\ln(q/p)\). With \(p=a-b\) and \(q=a+b\),
\[
\frac12\ln\frac{a+b}{a-b}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\tfrac12\log\bigl((a+b)/(a-b)\bigr)\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac12\log\frac{a+b}{a-b}.
\]
