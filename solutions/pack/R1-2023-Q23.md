## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin\frac{1}{x}\sin\frac{1}{3x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The product-to-sum formula gives
\[
\sin\frac{1}{x}\sin\frac{1}{3x}=\frac12\left(\cos\frac{2}{3x}-\cos\frac{4}{3x}\right).
\]
The worked PDF's alternative sets
\[
J(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-\cos\frac{\alpha}{x}\right)dx.
\]
The substitution \(x=\alpha/u\) produces \(J(\alpha)=\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos u)/u^{2}\,du\), and that integral equals \(\pi/2\), so \(J(\alpha)=\alpha\pi/2\). Therefore
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin\frac{1}{x}\sin\frac{1}{3x}\,dx=\frac12\bigl(J(4/3)-J(2/3)\bigr)=\frac12\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\frac{\pi}{6}.
\]
The last line of that alternative writes \(I=J(4/3)-J(2/3)=\pi/6\), dropping the factor \(1/2\) while still printing \(\pi/6\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi/6\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{6}.
\]
