## Problem

\[
\int\sqrt{1+e^x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF substitutes \(u=\sqrt{1+e^x}\), so \(e^x=u^2-1\) and \(e^x\,dx=2u\,du\). Then

\[
\int\sqrt{1+e^x}\,dx=\int\frac{2u^2}{u^2-1}\,du=\int\Bigl(2+\frac{2}{u^2-1}\Bigr)du=2u+\ln\frac{u-1}{u+1}+C.
\]

For every real \(x\), one has \(u>1\), so \((u-1)/(u+1)\) is positive and the absolute-value bars on the answers sheet are optional. The answers sheet prints

\[
\ln\left|\frac{\sqrt{1+e^x}-1}{\sqrt{1+e^x}+1}\right|+2\sqrt{1+e^x}+C,
\]

which is this antiderivative. The worked PDF also writes \(2u-2\operatorname{artanh} u\). That inverse hyperbolic tangent is not real for \(u>1\). The logarithmic expression is the real antiderivative printed on the answers sheet.

## Final answer

\[
\ln\left|\frac{\sqrt{1+e^x}-1}{\sqrt{1+e^x}+1}\right|+2\sqrt{1+e^x}+C.
\]
