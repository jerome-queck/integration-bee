## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,dx,
\]
where \(\sigma=(3\,1\,4)(5\,9\,2\,6)(8\,7\,0)\) permutes the digits \(\{0,1,\ldots,9\}\) and \(f\) applies \(\sigma\) to every decimal digit of \(x\).

## Solution

The three cycles use each digit once. Numbers in \([0,1]\) with two decimal expansions form a null set, so \(f\) is well defined almost everywhere. A cylinder of the first \(n\) digits has measure \(10^{-n}\), and \(\sigma\) permutes those cylinders. The cylinders generate the Lebesgue \(\sigma\)-algebra up to null sets, so \(f\) preserves Lebesgue measure. Therefore
\[
\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}x\,dx=\frac12.
\]
The solutions sheet reaches the same value by approximating \(f\) with permutations of the leading digits and passing to the limit.

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{1}{2}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy evaluates \(\int_{0}^{1}x\,dx=1/2\). Any permutation of a finite initial block of digits preserves the integral of \(x\) for the same cylinder reason: the average of the permuted leading digits, plus the average of the untouched tail, is still \(1/2\).
