## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2} \cot x \,\ln(\sec x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Use \(\ln(\sec x)=\frac12\ln(1+\tan^2 x)\) and the parameter integral from the worked PDF,

\[
I(a)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\cot x\,\ln(1+a\tan^2 x)\,dx,\qquad a>0.
\]

The original integral is \(\frac12 I(1)\). Differentiating under the integral sign gives

\[
I'(a)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\tan x}{1+a\tan^2 x}\,dx.
\]

The substitution \(u=\sin^2 x\) turns the integrand into \(\dfrac{1}{2\bigl(1+(a-1)u\bigr)}\), so

\[
I'(a)=\int_0^1\frac{du}{2\bigl(1+(a-1)u\bigr)}=\frac{\ln a}{2(a-1)}\qquad(a>0,\,a\neq 1).
\]

Also \(I(0)=0\). Therefore

\[
I(1)=\int_0^1\frac{\ln a}{2(a-1)}\,da.
\]

On \([0,1)\), \(\dfrac{1}{a-1}=-\sum_{n=0}^\infty a^n\), and \(\int_0^1 -a^n\ln a\,da=\dfrac{1}{(n+1)^2}\), so

\[
I(1)=\frac12\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{1}{(n+1)^2}=\frac12\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6}=\frac{\pi^2}{12}.
\]

The original integral is half of that. The worked PDF prints the series with coefficient \(\frac14\) and equates that sum directly to the original integral. The coefficient SymPy sums is \(\frac12\), which is \(I(1)\). Half of \(I(1)\) is the value printed on the answers sheet.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^2}{24}.
\]
