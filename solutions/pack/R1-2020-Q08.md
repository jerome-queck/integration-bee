## Problem

\[
\int_0^{1}\ln x\sin(\ln x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF reads the integral as the imaginary part of a parameter derivative. For \(a\) real,
\[
I(a)=\int_0^{1}x^{a}\,dx=\frac{1}{a+1},
\qquad
I'(a)=\int_0^{1}\ln x\cdot x^{a}\,dx=-\frac{1}{(a+1)^{2}}.
\]
The original integral is \(\operatorname{Im} I'(i)\). Now \((1+i)^{2}=2i\), so
\[
I'(i)=-\frac{1}{2i}=\frac{i}{2},
\]
and the imaginary part is \(1/2\). The answers sheet prints \(1/2\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{1}{2}.
\]
