## Problem

\[
\max_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\Biggl(\int_0^{\sin^2 k}\arcsin\sqrt{x}\,dx+\int_0^{\cos^2 k}\arccos\sqrt{x}\,dx\Biggr).
\]

## Solution

Call the expression \(f(k)\). The fundamental theorem gives

\[
f'(k)=2\sin k\cos k\Bigl(\arcsin|\sin k|-\arccos|\cos k|\Bigr).
\]

For \(t\in[0,1]\), \(\arccos\sqrt{1-t^2}=\arcsin t\), so \(\arcsin|\sin k|=\arccos|\cos k|\) and \(f'(k)=0\). Thus \(f\) is constant. At \(k=\pi/4\),

\[
f\Bigl(\frac\pi4\Bigr)=\int_0^{1/2}\bigl(\arcsin\sqrt{x}+\arccos\sqrt{x}\bigr)\,dx=\int_0^{1/2}\frac\pi2\,dx=\frac\pi4.
\]

The maximum is that constant. The mark scheme prints \(\pi/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac\pi4.
\]
