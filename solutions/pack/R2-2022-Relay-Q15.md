## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\cot^{-1}(x^{n/2})\,dx
\]

## Solution

For real \(n>2\), integrate by parts. The boundary term \(x\cot^{-1}(x^{n/2})\) vanishes, and
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\cot^{-1}(x^{n/2})\,dx=\frac{n}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n/2}}{1+x^{n}}\,dx.
\]
Set \(v=x^{n}\). The integral becomes
\[
\frac12\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{1/2+1/n-1}}{1+v}\,dv=\frac12\,B\Bigl(\frac12+\frac1n,\frac12-\frac1n\Bigr).
\]
Reflection gives \(\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin(\pi z)\), and \(\sin(\pi(1/2+1/n))=\cos(\pi/n)\). The value is \(\pi/(2\cos(\pi/n))\). The mark scheme prints that value.

For every real \(n\le2\) and \(x\ge1\), we have
\(x^{n/2}\le x\). Since \(\cot^{-1}\) decreases on positive arguments,
\[
\cot^{-1}(x^{n/2})\ge\cot^{-1}(x)
=\int_x^\infty\frac{dt}{1+t^2}
\ge\int_x^\infty\frac{dt}{2t^2}
=\frac1{2x}.
\]
The last function has a divergent integral, so the original integral is
\(+\infty\) throughout this branch, including \(n=0\) and negative \(n\).
Near zero, the integrand is bounded between \(0\) and \(\pi/2\) for every
real \(n\). The printed finite formula therefore needs the convergence
restriction \(n>2\), which the conditional answer makes explicit.

## Final answer

\[
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\pi}{2\cos(\pi/n)}, & n>2,\\[4pt]
+\infty, & n\le2,
\end{cases}
\qquad n\in\mathbb R.
\]
The mark scheme prints the first branch without a condition.
