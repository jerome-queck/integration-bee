## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{1-x^{2}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Split at \(1\) and substitute \(u=1/x\) on \([1,\infty)\). The two pieces agree, as a principal value at \(x=1\):
\[
\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{1-x^{2}}\,dx=2\int_0^{1}\frac{\ln x}{1-x^{2}}\,dx.
\]
Expand \(1/(1-x^{2})=\sum_{n\ge 0}x^{2n}\) on \([0,1)\) and integrate termwise:
\[
\int_0^{1}x^{2n}\ln x\,dx=-\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{2}}.
\]
Therefore the value is
\[
2\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{-1}{(2n+1)^{2}}=-2\cdot\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(-\pi^{2}/4\).

## Final answer

\[
-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{4}.
\]
