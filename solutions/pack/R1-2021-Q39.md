## Problem

\[
\int\frac{dx}{\sin^4 x+\cos^4 x}.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF rewrites the denominator as \(1-\frac12\sin^2 2x\):

\[
\sin^4 x+\cos^4 x=(\sin^2 x+\cos^2 x)^2-2\sin^2 x\cos^2 x=1-\frac12\sin^2 2x.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{1}{1-\frac12\sin^2 2x}=\frac{2}{2-\sin^2 2x}=\frac{2\sec^2 2x}{2+\tan^2 2x}.
\]

The substitution \(u=\tan 2x\) has derivative \(2\sec^2 2x\), so the integral is

\[
\int\frac{du}{2+u^2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\arctan\frac{u}{\sqrt{2}}+C.
\]

This is a local antiderivative on each interval where \(\tan 2x\) is continuous. It is the expression printed on the mark scheme.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\arctan\Bigl(\frac{\tan 2x}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigr)+C.
\]
