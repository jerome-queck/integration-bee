## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\ln(1-u)}{u}\,du\,dz.
\]

## Solution

For \(|u|<1\), \(\ln(1-u)=-\sum_{k\ge 1}u^{k}/k\), so
\[
\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\ln(1-u)}{u}\,du=-\sum_{k\ge 1}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{2}}.
\]
Integrating in \(z\) from \(0\) to \(1\),
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\ln(1-u)}{u}\,du\,dz=-\sum_{k\ge 1}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}.
\]
The partial fraction
\[
\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}=-\frac1k+\frac{1}{k^{2}}+\frac{1}{k+1}
\]
sums to \(\pi^{2}/6-1\), because \(\sum 1/k^{2}=\pi^{2}/6\) and \(\sum(1/(k+1)-1/k)=-1\). The double integral is therefore \(1-\pi^{2}/6\).

## Final answer

\[
1-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy simplifies \(1/(k^{2}(k+1))-(-1/k+1/k^{2}+1/(k+1))\) to \(0\), and sums \(\sum_{k\ge 1}1/(k^{2}(k+1))=\pi^{2}/6-1\).
