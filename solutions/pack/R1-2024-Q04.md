## Problem

\[
\int \sin^2+\cos^2\,ds.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF treats the glyphs as products of letters rather than the sine and cosine functions. Read \(\sin^2\) as the product of the letter \(s\) with \(\mathrm{in}^2\), and read \(\cos^2\) as the square of the symbol \(\mathrm{cos}\). Integrate the first factor in the letter \(s\) and the second in the symbol \(\mathrm{cos}\):

\[
\int s\,\mathrm{in}^2\,ds+\int\mathrm{cos}^2\,d(\mathrm{cos})
=\frac{s^2\mathrm{in}^2}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{cos}^3}{3}+C.
\]

That is the antiderivative printed in the worked PDF and in the mark scheme.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{s^2\mathrm{in}^2}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{cos}^3}{3}+C.
\]
