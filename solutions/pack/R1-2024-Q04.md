## Problem

\[
\int \sin^2+\cos^2\,ds.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF treats the glyphs as products of the five letters
\(s,i,n,c,o\). Thus the integrand is \(s\,i\,n^2+c\,o\,s^2\).
Only \(s\) is the integration variable; \(i,n,c,o\) are constants:

\[
\int (s\,i\,n^2+c\,o\,s^2)\,ds
=\frac{i\,n^2s^2}{2}+\frac{c\,o\,s^3}{3}+C.
\]

That is the antiderivative printed in the worked PDF and in the mark scheme.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{i\,n^2s^2}{2}+\frac{c\,o\,s^3}{3}+C.
\]

## SymPy self-check

The transcript renames \(s\) to \(x\) and preserves the constant letter
\(i\) as `Symbol('i')`, separately from SymPy's imaginary unit. The ordinary
antiderivative gate differentiates the whole printed polynomial in that
single integration variable and recovers the integrand exactly.
