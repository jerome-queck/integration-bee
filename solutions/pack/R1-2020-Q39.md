## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\frac{1}{x\sqrt{1-x^2}}\ln\frac{2+x}{2-x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF introduces, for \(0\le a<1\),

\[
I(a)=\int_0^1\frac{1}{x\sqrt{1-x^2}}\ln\frac{1+ax}{1-ax}\,dx,
\]

so the requested integral is \(I(1/2)\). Differentiating in \(a\) and setting \(x=\sin\theta\) produces

\[
I'(a)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{2\,d\theta}{1-a^2\sin^2\theta}.
\]

The substitution \(u=\tan\theta\) converts this to

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{2\,du}{1+(1-a^2)u^2}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{1-a^2}}.
\]

Since \(I(0)=0\),

\[
I\Bigl(\frac12\Bigr)=\int_0^{1/2}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{1-a^2}}\,da=\pi\arcsin\frac12=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\]

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\]
