## Problem

\[
\int\frac{1}{x^2-x\sqrt{x^2-1}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF sets \(x=\sec\theta\), so \(dx=\sec\theta\tan\theta\,d\theta\) and the integrand becomes \(\sin\theta/(1-\sin\theta)\). Writing that as \(-1+1/(1-\sin\theta)\) and multiplying by the conjugate produces
\[
-\theta+\tan\theta+\sec\theta+C=- \operatorname{arcsec} x+\sqrt{x^2-1}+x+C.
\]
The answers sheet prints the equivalent antiderivative \(x+\sqrt{x^2-1}-\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}\).

## Final answer

\[
x+\sqrt{x^2-1}-\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}.
\]
