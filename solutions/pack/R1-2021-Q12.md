## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x^n}\,dx,\qquad 0<n<2.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF writes \(\sin x=(e^{ix}-e^{-ix})/(2i)\) and substitutes in each exponential integral. The result is the standard Mellin evaluation

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x^n}\,dx=\sin\Bigl(\frac{(1-n)\pi}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma(1-n).
\]

The mark scheme also prints \(\cos(n\pi/2)\,\Gamma(1-n)\) and \(\pi\csc(n\pi/2)/(2\Gamma(n))\), and states that the three expressions are equal. The first two agree because \(\sin((1-n)\pi/2)=\cos(n\pi/2)\). The reflection formula \(\Gamma(n)\Gamma(1-n)=\pi/\sin(n\pi)\) and the double-angle identity \(\sin(n\pi)=2\sin(n\pi/2)\cos(n\pi/2)\) turn the second expression into the third, for \(0<n<2\) with \(n\neq 1\). At \(n=1\) each form has the limit \(\pi/2\).

## Final answer

The mark scheme prints three equal forms on \(0<n<2\), \(n\neq 1\). At \(n=1\), \(\cos(n\pi/2)\Gamma(1-n)\) is an indeterminate product. The catalog stores the reflection form, which is defined at \(n=1\) and equals \(\pi/2\) there:

\[
\frac{\pi}{2\Gamma(n)\sin(n\pi/2)}.
\]
