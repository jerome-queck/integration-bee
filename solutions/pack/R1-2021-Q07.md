## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\ln x}{x^2+2x+2}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(u=2/x\) preserves the denominator \(x^2+2x+2\) and replaces \(\ln x\) by \(\ln 2-\ln u\). The original integral \(I\) therefore equals \(\int_0^\infty(\ln 2-\ln x)/(x^2+2x+2)\,dx\). Adding the two copies cancels the logarithm and leaves
\[
2I=\ln 2\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2+2x+2}=\ln 2\cdot\frac\pi4,
\]
because the antiderivative is \(\arctan(x+1)\). Thus \(I=\pi\ln 2/8\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi\ln 2/8\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi\ln 2}{8}.
\]
