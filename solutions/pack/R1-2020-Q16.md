## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi}\frac{\ln(1+k\cos x)}{\cos x}\,dx,\qquad 0<k<1.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF sets \(I(k)\) equal to the integral and differentiates under the sign:
\[
I'(k)=\int_0^{\pi}\frac{dx}{1+k\cos x}.
\]
The Weierstrass substitution \(t=\tan(x/2)\) produces
\[
I'(k)=\int_0^{\infty}\frac{2\,dt}{(1+k)+(1-k)t^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}}},
\]
because \(\arctan t\to\pi/2\) at infinity. Also \(I(0)=0\), so \(I(k)=\pi\arcsin k\). The answers sheet prints \(\pi\arcsin k\).

## Final answer

\[
\pi\arcsin k.
\]
