## Problem

\[
\int_0^{1}\frac{x-1}{(x+1)\ln x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF sets
\[
I(a)=\int_0^{1}\frac{x^{a}-1}{(x+1)\ln x}\,dx
\]
and differentiates under the integral sign:
\[
I'(a)=\int_0^{1}\frac{x^{a}}{1+x}\,dx=\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{a+n+1}.
\]
The original integral is \(I(1)=\int_0^{1}I'(t)\,dt\). Integrating the series produces
\[
\sum_{n\ge 0}(-1)^{n}\ln\frac{n+2}{n+1}=\ln\prod_{n\ge 0}\frac{(2n+2)^{2}}{(2n+1)(2n+3)}.
\]
That product is the Wallis product for \(\pi/2\). The mark scheme prints \(\ln(\pi/2)\), which is \(-\ln 2+\ln\pi\).

## Final answer

\[
\ln\dfrac{\pi}{2}.
\]
