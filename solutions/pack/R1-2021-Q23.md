## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\ln(\cos x)}{\sin x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The solutions PDF introduces
\[
I(a)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\ln(1-a\sin^2 x)}{\sin x}\,dx.
\]
Differentiating in \(a\) and integrating the resulting rational function of \(\cos x\) gives
\[
I'(a)=-\frac{\arcsin\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{a}\sqrt{1-a}}.
\]
The endpoint values satisfy \(I(0)=0\) and \(I(1)=2\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\cos x)/\sin x\,dx\). Therefore the original integral is
\[
\frac12\int_0^1 I'(a)\,da=-\frac12\Bigl[\arcsin^2\sqrt{a}\Bigr]_0^1=-\frac{\pi^2}{8}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(-\pi^2/8\).

## Final answer

\[
-\frac{\pi^2}{8}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

The series \(\sum_{k\ge 0}1/(2k+1)^2=\pi^2/8\) agrees.
