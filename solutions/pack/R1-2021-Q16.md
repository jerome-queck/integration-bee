## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\{\tan x\}}{\tan x}\,dx,
\]

where \(\{u\}=u-\lfloor u\rfloor\).

## Solution

The substitution \(u=\tan x\) gives

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\{u\}}{u(1+u^2)}\,du
=\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{1+u^2}-\sum_{n=1}^\infty n\int_n^{n+1}\frac{du}{u(1+u^2)}.
\]

The first integral is \(\pi/2\). The inner antiderivative is \(\frac12\ln\bigl(u^2/(1+u^2)\bigr)\), so the sum is

\[
\frac12\sum_{n=1}^\infty n\Biggl(\ln\frac{(n+1)^2}{1+(n+1)^2}-\ln\frac{n^2}{1+n^2}\Biggr).
\]

Re-indexing the worked PDF produces

\[
\frac\pi2-\frac12\ln 2-\frac12\ln\prod_{n=2}^\infty\Bigl(1+\frac1{n^2}\Bigr).
\]

The sine product \(\sin z=z\prod_{n\ge 1}(1-z^2/(n^2\pi^2))\) at \(z=i\pi\) gives

\[
\prod_{n=2}^\infty\Bigl(1+\frac1{n^2}\Bigr)=\frac{\sinh\pi}{2\pi},
\]

and the displayed line above is

\[
\frac12\Biggl(\pi-\ln\frac{\sinh\pi}{\pi}\Biggr).
\]

The last line of the worked PDF replaces that with \(\pi/2-\frac12\ln(\sinh\pi/(2\pi))\), which is larger by \(\frac12\ln 2\). The mark scheme prints the value from the product line above.

## Final answer

\[
\frac12\Biggl(\pi-\ln\frac{\sinh\pi}{\pi}\Biggr).
\]

## SymPy self-check

`verify/pack_handoff_e_series.py` first checks the tangent substitution and each floor interval. For \(h(k)=\ln(1+k^{-2})\), finite summation by parts gives

\[
\sum_{k=1}^N k\bigl(h(k)-h(k+1)\bigr)
=\ln\prod_{k=1}^N(1+k^{-2})-N\ln(1+(N+1)^{-2}).
\]

SymPy evaluates the finite product as a rising-factorial quotient, takes its limit, and simplifies the gamma reflection value to \(\sinh\pi/\pi\). It also verifies that the boundary term vanishes. The transformed integrand's tail is bounded by \(\int_{N+1}^\infty u^{-3}\,du\), so these finite endpoints recover the improper integral.
