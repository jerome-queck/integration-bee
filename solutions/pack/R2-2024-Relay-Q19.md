## Problem

\[
\int_{1/e}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\ln x+1}}{x^{2}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The lower limit is \(1/e\). Set \(y=\ln x+1\), so \(x=e^{y-1}\) and \(dx/x^{2}=e^{1-y}\,dy\). The limits run from \(0\) to \(\infty\), and the integral becomes
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{1-y}\sqrt{y}\,dy=e\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{1/2}e^{-y}\,dy=e\,\Gamma(3/2).
\]
The gamma value \(\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt{\pi}/2\) is the other form printed on the mark scheme.

## Final answer

\[
e\,\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{3}{2}\Bigr)=\dfrac{e\sqrt{\pi}}{2}.
\]
