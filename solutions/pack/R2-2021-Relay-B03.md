# R2-2021-Relay-B03

- Problem: `sources/past/r2/2021/Shuttle_2021_R2_UK_University_Integration_Bee.pdf`, PDF p. 3 (shuttle B2-B3).
- Printed answer: `sources/past/r2/2021/Mark_Scheme_2021_R2_UK_University_Integration_Bee.pdf`, PDF p. 4 (shuttle B2-B3).
- Worked source: derived in this note; the mark scheme prints the final value.
- Catalog contract: the signed infinite value, under the explicit limiting interpretation below.

## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln(b(x+1))}{x^{2}+1}\,dx,
\]

## Solution

The question asks to pass that value on. Shuttle B1 passes zero; B2 evaluates to \(13\pi\), whose second digit is zero, and therefore passes \(b=0\). Both pass-ons are re-derived by the certificate, including rational bounds on \(\pi\) that fix the second digit.

The problem PDF does **not** print \(b\to0^+\). Its literal substitution would contain \(\log 0\), and the mark scheme explicitly gives \(-\infty\). The catalog certifies this extended-value reading as
\[
\lim_{b\to0^+}\int_0^1\frac{\log(b(x+1))}{1+x^2}\,dx=-\infty.
\]
It does not claim that \(\log 0\) is a finite real number.

Split the integrand as \(\ln b+\ln(x+1)\). Then
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{x^{2}+1}=\frac{\pi}{4},
\]
while \(\ln(x+1)/(x^{2}+1)\) is continuous on \([0,1]\), between zero and \(\log 2\). Hence the integral lies between \((\pi/4)\log b\) and \((\pi/4)\log b+\log 2\). Both bounds tend to \(-\infty\) as \(b\to0^+\), proving the printed extended value without subtracting infinities.

## Final answer

\[
-\infty.
\]
