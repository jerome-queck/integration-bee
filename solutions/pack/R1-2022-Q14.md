## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\ln(\sin x)}{\cos^{2}x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Integrate by parts with \(u=\ln(\sin x)\) and \(dv=\sec^{2}x\,dx\). Then \(du=\cot x\,dx\) and \(v=\tan x\), so
\[
\int\frac{\ln(\sin x)}{\cos^{2}x}\,dx=\tan x\ln(\sin x)-\int 1\,dx.
\]
The boundary term \(\tan x\ln(\sin x)\) vanishes at both endpoints of \((0,\pi/2)\): near \(0\) it behaves like \(x\ln x\), and near \(\pi/2\) like \(\varepsilon\ln(1)\). The remaining integral is
\[
-\int_0^{\pi/2}1\,dx=-\frac\pi2.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(-\pi/2\).

## Final answer

\[
-\dfrac{\pi}{2}.
\]
