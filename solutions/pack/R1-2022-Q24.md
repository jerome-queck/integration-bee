## Problem

\[
\int_0^{2\pi}e^{\cos x}\cos(nx-\sin x)\,dx,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF takes the real part of a geometric series, for \(n\) a natural number (including the \(n=0\) case the same expansion covers):

\[
e^{\cos x}\cos(nx-\sin x)=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e^{inx}e^{e^{-ix}}\bigr)
=\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{e^{i(n-k)x}}{k!}.
\]

Integrating term by term, \(\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i(n-k)x}\,dx\) is \(2\pi\) when \(k=n\) and \(0\) otherwise. The integral is \(2\pi/n!\).

The mark scheme prints \(2\pi/n!\) with the problem's hypothesis \(n\in\mathbb{Z}\). For a negative integer the same series has no term \(k=n\), so the integral is \(0\), and \(n!\) is not defined. The printed factorial expression is the value for \(n=0,1,2,\ldots\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{2\pi}{n!}\qquad(n=0,1,2,\ldots).
\]

For \(n\) a negative integer the integral is \(0\).
