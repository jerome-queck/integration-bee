## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x\sin 2x}{x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The product-to-sum formula in the solutions PDF gives
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x\sin 2x}{x}\,dx=\frac12\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x-\cos 3x}{x}\,dx.
\]
That PDF sets
\[
I(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x-\cos 3x}{x}e^{-tx}\,dx,
\]
so the original integral is \(I(0)/2\). Differentiating under the integral sign,
\[
I'(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\cos 3x-\cos x)e^{-tx}\,dx=\frac{t}{t^{2}+9}-\frac{t}{t^{2}+1}.
\]
An antiderivative is \(\frac12\ln((t^{2}+9)/(t^{2}+1))\). It tends to \(0\) as \(t\to\infty\), and \(I(0)=\ln 3\). Therefore the original integral is \((\ln 3)/2\). The mark scheme prints \((\ln 3)/2\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\ln 3}{2}.
\]
