## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}(x+\sin x)\,e^{-x/2}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The problem sheet writes the exponential factor as \(1/\sqrt{e^{x}}\), which is \(e^{-x/2}\). The worked PDF splits the integral:
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}x e^{-x/2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin x\,e^{-x/2}\,dx.
\]
The first is the Gamma integral for scale \(1/2\): with \(t=x/2\), it equals \(4\). The second is the Laplace transform of \(\sin x\) at \(s=1/2\),
\[
\frac{1}{(1/2)^{2}+1}=\frac{4}{5}.
\]
The displayed line \(4+=24/5\) in the worked PDF is this sum. The mark scheme prints \(24/5\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{24}{5}.
\]
