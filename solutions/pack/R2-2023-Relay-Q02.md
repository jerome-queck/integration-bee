## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sin(2^{n}x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The PDF has a superscript \(n\) on \(2\). The earlier OCR-based reading
\(\sin(2nx)\) was incorrect; density of all irrational rotations does not
prove the printed doubling-orbit statement.

Set \(y=x/\pi\). For each integer \(m\ge2\), consider the binary word
\(01\) followed by \(m-2\) zeros. It specifies the interval
\([1/4,1/4+2^{-m})\). Among the first \(k\) disjoint blocks of \(m\) binary
digits, the set avoiding this word has measure
\[
\frac{(2^m-1)^k}{2^{mk}}=(1-2^{-m})^k\longrightarrow0.
\]
Binary endpoint ambiguities form a countable set. Consequently almost every
\(y\) has the word in some block; shifting that block to the start means
\(\{2^{jm}y\}\in[1/4,1/4+2^{-m})\) for some \(j\ge0\).
The corresponding sine term, with \(n=jm+1\), satisfies
\[
\sin(2^n x)\ge1-\frac12(2\pi\,2^{-m})^2.
\]
Take the countable intersection of these full-measure sets over \(m\), then
let \(m\to\infty\). Since every sine term is at most \(1\), the supremum
equals \(1\) almost everywhere. It is continuous at each such point: a
continuous sine term gives an arbitrarily close lower bound and \(1\) is a
global upper bound. Thus its discontinuities form a null set, so it is also
Riemann-integrable. Its integral is \(\pi\), as printed in the mark scheme.

The transcript uses the exact standard-operator representation
\[
\sup_{j\ge1}\sin(2^j x)
=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
\log\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{N\sin(2^j x)}\right).
\]
If \(M_N\) is the maximum of the first \(N\) sine terms, the expression
inside the limit lies between \(M_N\) and \(M_N+\log(N)/N\). This proves
the identity at every point, without replacing the original problem by a
constant integrand. Including \(n=0\) in the convention for \(\mathbb N\)
does not change the almost-everywhere supremum or the integral.

## Final answer

\[
\pi.
\]
