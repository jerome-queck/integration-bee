## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sin(2nx)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

If \(x/\pi\) is irrational, the sequence of fractional parts \(\{nx/\pi\}\) is dense in \([0,1]\) by Dirichlet's approximation theorem. Then \(2nx\) is dense modulo \(2\pi\), so \(\sin(2nx)\) comes arbitrarily close to \(1\) and the supremum is \(1\). The set of \(x\) for which \(x/\pi\) is rational is countable, hence has measure zero and does not affect the integral. The integrand equals \(1\) almost everywhere, and the integral is \(\pi\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi\).

## Final answer

\pi.
