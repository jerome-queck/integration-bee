## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\infty}\lfloor x\rfloor e^{-x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Split the integral at the integers. On \([n,n+1)\) the floor is \(n\), and the \(n=0\) piece vanishes, so
\[
\sum_{n\ge 1}n\int_n^{n+1}e^{-x}\,dx=\bigl(1-e^{-1}\bigr)\sum_{n\ge 1}n e^{-n}.
\]
The arithmetico-geometric sum \(\sum_{n\ge 1}nr^{n}=r/(1-r)^{2}\) at \(r=e^{-1}\) gives \(1/(e-1)\). The mark scheme prints \(1/(e-1)\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{1}{e-1}.
\]
