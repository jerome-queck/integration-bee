## Problem

Shuttle A3 asks for four times its integral on p. 2; the mark scheme records that transmitted value on p. 4. The number received from shuttle A3 is \(c=\pi\). Evaluate
\[
\int_{0}^{c}x\ln(\sin x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The received limit is \(\pi\). Write \(J=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin x)\,dx\). On \((0,\pi/2)\),
\[
\sin(2x)=2\sin x\cos x,
\]
so \(\ln(\sin(2x))=\ln(\sin x)+\ln(\cos x)+\ln 2\). The substitution \(x\mapsto\pi/2-x\) shows that the sine and cosine integrals are equal, and \(\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin(2x))\,dx=J\). Therefore \(2J=J-(\pi/2)\ln 2\), and \(J=-(\pi/2)\ln 2\).

For \(I=\int_{0}^{\pi}x\ln(\sin x)\,dx\), the reflection \(x\mapsto\pi-x\) gives \(2I=\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\ln(\sin x)\,dx\). The substitution \(x\mapsto\pi-x\) also shows that the halves \([0,\pi/2]\) and \([\pi/2,\pi]\) contribute the same value \(J\). Thus \(2I=\pi\cdot 2J\), and
\[
I=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\ln 2.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(-\tfrac12\pi^{2}\ln 2\).

## Final answer

\[
-\frac12\pi^{2}\ln 2.
\]
