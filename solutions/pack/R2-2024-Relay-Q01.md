## Problem

\[
\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{x^{2}\cos x}{1+e^{x}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The numerator \(x^{2}\cos x\) is even and the factor \(1/(1+e^{x})\) pairs with its reflection:
\[
\frac{1}{1+e^{x}}+\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}=1.
\]
Adding the integrand to its reflection therefore replaces the fraction by \(1\), and symmetry cuts the interval in half:
\[
\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{x^{2}\cos x}{1+e^{x}}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x^{2}\cos x\,dx.
\]
Integration by parts gives the antiderivative \((x^{2}-2)\sin x+2x\cos x\). At \(\pi/2\) this is \(\pi^{2}/4-2\), and at \(0\) it is \(0\). The mark scheme prints the same value as \((\pi^{2}-8)/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi^{2}-8}{4}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy differentiates \((x^{2}-2)\sin x+2x\cos x\) back to \(x^{2}\cos x\), evaluates it from \(0\) to \(\pi/2\) as \(\pi^{2}/4-2\), and checks that this equals \((\pi^{2}-8)/4\).
