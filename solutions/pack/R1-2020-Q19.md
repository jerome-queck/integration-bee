## Problem

\[
\int_0^1 x^{-x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Write \(x^{-x}=e^{-x\ln x}\). On \((0,1]\),

\[
e^{-x\ln x}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{x^n(-\ln x)^n}{n!}.
\]

Every term is nonnegative, so monotone convergence justifies the worked PDF's termwise integration. The substitution \(u=-\ln x\) sends \(x=0\) to \(\infty\) and \(x=1\) to \(0\), and the \(n\)th term becomes

\[
\frac{1}{n!}\int_0^\infty e^{-(n+1)u}u^n\,du=\frac{1}{(n+1)^{n+1}}.
\]

Reindex by \(k=n+1\). The resulting series converges, since \(k^{-k}\le 2^{-k}\) for \(k\ge2\).

## Final answer

\[
\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{1}{k^k}.
\]

This is the series printed on the 2020 answers sheet.
