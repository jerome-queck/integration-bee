## Problem

Evaluate \(\int_{0}^{1}(\ln x)^{2}\,dx\). Pass on the square of the value.

## Solution

Integration by parts with \(u=(\ln x)^{2}\) and \(dv=dx\) gives the boundary term \(x(\ln x)^{2}\), which tends to \(0\) at both endpoints, and
\[
\int_{0}^{1}(\ln x)^{2}\,dx=-2\int_{0}^{1}\ln x\,dx.
\]
A second integration by parts gives \(\int_{0}^{1}\ln x\,dx=-1\), so the integral equals \(2\). The value passed on is \(2^{2}=4\). The mark scheme prints \(4\).

## Final answer

\[
4.
\]
