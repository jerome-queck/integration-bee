## Problem

\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)\prod_{n=0}^{N}\Biggl(x^{2^{n}}+\frac{\{\sinh^{2}(x^{2^{n}})\}}{\{\cosh^{2}(x^{2^{n}})\}}\Biggr)\,dx,
\]
where \(\{\,\cdot\,\}\) is the fractional part.

## Solution

The identity \(\cosh^{2}y-\sinh^{2}y=1\) says that \(\cosh^{2}y\) and \(\sinh^{2}y\) differ by the integer \(1\), so they have the same fractional part. Wherever that fractional part is nonzero the ratio is \(1\), and the zeros are isolated. The product collapses to
\[
(1-x)\prod_{n=0}^{N}(1+x^{2^{n}})=1-x^{2^{N+1}}.
\]
On \([0,1]\) the integrand tends pointwise to \(1\) except at \(x=1\), and it is bounded by \(1\). The limit of the integrals is \(1\).

## Final answer

\[
1.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy checks \(\cosh^{2}y-\sinh^{2}y=1\), the fractional-part period, and the identity \((1-z)(1+z)=1-z^2\). The last identity proves the finite product formula by induction, with base \(N=0\) and step \(z=x^{2^{N+1}}\). The gate checks the primitive of \(1-x^m\) for \(m>0\), and then the exact limit
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(1-\frac1{2^{N+1}+1}\right)=1.
\]
Each denominator's zero set is finite on \((0,1)\), because \(\cosh^2(x^{2^n})\) is strictly increasing. Their union over \(n\) is countable, so filling the ratio's removable values by \(1\) does not change any integral. The finite telescoped integrands lie in \([0,1]\), proving the dominated-convergence exchange used by the stored literal limit.
