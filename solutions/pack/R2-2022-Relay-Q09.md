## Problem

Solve
\[
f(x)-\int_{0}^{x}f(t)\,dt=1.
\]

## Solution

In the class of continuously differentiable functions on \(\mathbb R\), the integral vanishes at \(x=0\), so \(f(0)=1\). Differentiating both sides gives \(f'(x)-f(x)=0\). Thus
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(e^{-x}f(x)\bigr)=e^{-x}\bigl(f'(x)-f(x)\bigr)=0.
\]
The expression \(e^{-x}f(x)\) is constant on \(\mathbb R\), and its value at zero is \(1\). This proves uniqueness: \(f(x)=e^{x}\). Substituting \(e^{x}\) back satisfies the integral equation. The mark scheme prints \(f(x)=e^{x}\).

## Final answer

\[
f(x)=e^{x}.
\]
