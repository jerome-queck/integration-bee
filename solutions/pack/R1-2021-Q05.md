## Problem

\[
\int_0^{4}\frac{\ln x}{\sqrt{4x-x^{2}}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Complete the square in the root: \(4x-x^{2}=4-(x-2)^{2}\). The substitution \(x-2=2\sin\theta\) runs from \(\theta=-\pi/2\) to \(\pi/2\), and the Jacobian cancels the square root, leaving
\[
I=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\ln(2+2\sin\theta)\,d\theta.
\]
Shift \(\varphi=\theta+\pi/2\). Then \(2+2\sin\theta=4\sin^{2}(\varphi/2)\), and
\[
I=\int_0^{\pi}\bigl(\ln 4+2\ln\sin(\varphi/2)\bigr)\,d\varphi=\pi\ln 4+4\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin u)\,du.
\]
The worked PDF uses the standard evaluation \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin u)\,du=-(\pi/2)\ln 2\). Substituting it gives
\[
I=\pi\ln 4+4\bigl(-\tfrac\pi2\ln 2\bigr)=0.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(0\).
