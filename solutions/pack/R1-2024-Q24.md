## Problem

\[
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\ln\bigl((1+x)(1+x^2)(1+x^4)\cdots(1+x^{2^{2024}})\bigr)}{x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Multiply and divide by \(1-x\) inside the logarithm. The difference of squares collapses the product:

\[
(1-x)\prod_{k=0}^{2024}(1+x^{2^k})=1-x^{2^{2025}}.
\]

The integral splits as

\[
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\ln(1-x^{2^{2025}})}{x}\,dx-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\ln(1-x)}{x}\,dx.
\]

The first integrand is odd, and the singularity at \(0\) is removable, so that integral is \(0\). For the second, \(\ln(1-x)=-\sum_{n\ge 1}x^n/n\), and

\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n}\int_{-1}^{1}x^{n-1}\,dx=\sum_{m=0}^\infty\frac{2}{(2m+1)^2}=2\Biggl(\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\frac{\pi^2}{24}\Biggr)=\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\]

The worked PDF writes the odd denominators as \(2n+1\) starting at \(n=1\), which would drop the \(m=0\) term. The next line of that PDF is the full odd sum \(\sum n^{-2}-\sum(2n)^{-2}\), and that is the sum evaluated here. The mark scheme prints \(\pi^2/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\]
