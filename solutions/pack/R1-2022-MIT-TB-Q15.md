## Problem

\[
\int\sqrt{1+\sin\frac{x}{2}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The half-angle identity
\[
1+\sin\frac{x}{2}=\Bigl(\sin\frac{x}{4}+\cos\frac{x}{4}\Bigr)^{2}
\]
makes the positive square root equal to \(\sin(x/4)+\cos(x/4)\) wherever that sum is nonnegative, including an open interval about \(x=0\). On each such interval
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\Bigl(4\Bigl(\sin\frac{x}{4}-\cos\frac{x}{4}\Bigr)\Bigr)=\sin\frac{x}{4}+\cos\frac{x}{4}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(4(\sin(x/4)-\cos(x/4))\).

## Final answer

\[
4\Bigl(\sin\dfrac{x}{4}-\cos\dfrac{x}{4}\Bigr).
\]
