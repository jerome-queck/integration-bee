## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\ln(\sin x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

On \((0,\pi)\),
\[
\ln(\sin x)=-\ln 2-\sum_{k\ge 1}\frac{\cos(2kx)}{k}.
\]
The constant term contributes
\[
(-\ln 2)\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\,dx=-\frac{\pi^{2}\ln 2}{8}.
\]
For the cosine terms,
\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos(2kx)\,dx=\frac{(-1)^{k}-1}{4k^{2}},
\]
which vanishes for even \(k\). The Fourier series enters with a minus sign. For odd \(k\) the moment is negative, so the contribution of \(k=2m+1\) is \(1/(2(2m+1)^{3})\). Summing those terms gives
\[
\sum_{m\ge 0}\frac{1}{2(2m+1)^{3}}=\frac{7}{16}\zeta(3),
\]
because \(\sum_{m\ge 0}1/(2m+1)^{3}=(7/8)\zeta(3)\). Adding the constant term gives
\[
\frac{7}{16}\zeta(3)-\frac{\pi^{2}\ln 2}{8}.
\]
The mark scheme prints that value.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{7}{16}\zeta(3)-\frac{\pi^{2}\ln 2}{8}.
\]
