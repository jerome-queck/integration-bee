## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{e}f(e^{x})\,dx,
\]
where \(f(x)\) is the unique \(c\ge 1\) with \(c^{c}=x\).

## Solution

The relation \(c^{c}=e^{x}\) is \(x=c\ln c\). Then \(dx=(\ln c+1)\,dc\). The endpoint \(x=0\) forces \(c=1\), and \(x=e\) forces \(c=e\). The integrand \(f(e^{x})\) is this \(c\), so the integral becomes
\[
\int_{1}^{e}(c\ln c+c)\,dc=\Bigl[\frac{c^{2}\ln c}{2}+\frac{c^{2}}{4}\Bigr]_{1}^{e}=\frac{3e^{2}}{4}-\frac14.
\]

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{3e^{2}}{4}-\dfrac{1}{4}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy differentiates \(c^{2}\ln c/2+c^{2}/4\) back to \(c\ln c+c\), and the endpoint difference is \(3e^{2}/4-1/4\).
