## Problem

\[
I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\left(\frac{1+\sin x}{1-\sin x}\right)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Put \(x=\pi/2-2\theta\). For \(0<\theta\leq\pi/4\),
\[
\frac{1+\sin x}{1-\sin x}
=\frac{1+\cos(2\theta)}{1-\cos(2\theta)}
=\cot^2\theta>0.
\]
Hence the real logarithm is \(-2\ln\tan\theta\), and
\[
I=-4\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\tan\theta\,d\theta
=-4\int_0^1\frac{\ln u}{1+u^2}\,du
=4\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2}=4G.
\]
Here \(u=\tan\theta\). The geometric expansion can be integrated termwise
because the integrated absolute terms sum to \(\sum(2k+1)^{-2}<\infty\).
This also establishes integrability of the endpoint logarithm.

## Final answer

\[
\boxed{4G}.
\]
