## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln\Biggl(\frac{1+\sin x}{1-\sin x}\Biggr)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

On \((0,\pi/2)\),
\[
\frac{1+\sin x}{1-\sin x}=\tan^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{x}{2}\Bigr),
\]
so the integrand is \(2\ln\tan(\pi/4+x/2)\). The substitution \(u=\pi/4+x/2\) gives
\[
I=4\int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2}\ln(\tan u)\,du.
\]
Then \(w=\tan u\) produces \(4\int_{1}^{\infty}\ln w/(1+w^{2})\,dw\). The integral of \(\ln w/(1+w^{2})\) over \((0,\infty)\) is \(0\), because \(w\mapsto 1/w\) negates it. Hence
\[
I=-4\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln w}{1+w^{2}}\,dw.
\]
The geometric series \(\sum_{n\ge 0}(-1)^{n}w^{2n}\) and \(\int_{0}^{1}w^{2n}\ln w\,dw=-1/(2n+1)^{2}\) give
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln w}{1+w^{2}}\,dw=-G,
\]
where \(G=\sum_{n\ge 0}(-1)^{n}/(2n+1)^{2}\) is Catalan's constant. Therefore \(I=4G\). The mark scheme prints \(4G\).

## Final answer

\[
4G.
\]
