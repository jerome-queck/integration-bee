## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\frac{\sec^2 x-2}{\tan x-1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

On \([0,\pi/4)\), \(\tan x\neq 1\) and

\[
\sec^2 x-2=\tan^2 x-1=(\tan x-1)(\tan x+1),
\]

so the integrand is \(\ln(\tan x+1)\). The worked PDF continues

\[
\ln(\tan x+1)=\ln(\sin x+\cos x)-\ln(\cos x)=\ln\bigl(\sqrt{2}\cos(\pi/4-x)\bigr)-\ln(\cos x).
\]

The integral of \(\ln\sqrt{2}\) over an interval of length \(\pi/4\) is \((\pi/8)\ln 2\). The substitution \(u=\pi/4-x\) shows that \(\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\cos(\pi/4-x)\,dx\) equals \(\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\cos x\,dx\), so those two terms cancel.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi}{8}\ln 2.
\]
