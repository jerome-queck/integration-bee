## Problem

\[
\int_0^\pi\ln(a+b\cos x)\,dx,\qquad a>b.
\]

The worked PDF carries the argument through with \(a>b>0\), which keeps every logarithm and \(\operatorname{acosh}(a/b)\) real.

## Solution

Differentiate in \(a\):

\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\int_0^\pi\ln(a+b\cos x)\,dx=\int_0^\pi\frac{dx}{a+b\cos x}.
\]

The Weierstrass substitution in the worked PDF evaluates the right-hand side as \(\pi/\sqrt{a^2-b^2}\). Integrating in \(a\) produces

\[
\pi\operatorname{acosh}\frac{a}{b}+C.
\]

The constant is fixed by \(a=b>0\). Then

\[
\int_0^\pi\ln\bigl(b(1+\cos x)\bigr)\,dx=\pi\ln b+\int_0^\pi\ln(1+\cos x)\,dx=\pi\ln\frac{b}{2},
\]

after the same log-sine evaluation used for problem 21 of this paper, so \(C=\pi\ln(b/2)\). The identity

\[
\operatorname{acosh}\frac{a}{b}=\ln\Bigl(\frac{a}{b}+\sqrt{\Bigl(\frac{a}{b}\Bigr)^2-1}\Bigr)
\]

turns the worked expression into the form printed on the answers sheet.

## Final answer

\[
\pi\ln\frac{a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}{2},\qquad a>b>0.
\]
