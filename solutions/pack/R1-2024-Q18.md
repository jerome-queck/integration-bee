## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The reflection \(u=\pi/2-x\) replaces \(\ln(\sin x)\) by \(\ln(\cos x)\). Adding the two copies,
\[
2I=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin x\cos x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln\frac{\sin 2x}{2}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin 2x)\,dx-\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2.
\]
Split \(\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin 2x)\,dx\) at \(\pi/4\) and fold each half back to \([0,\pi/2]\). The solutions PDF records that this integral equals \(I\). Therefore \(2I=I-(\pi/2)\ln 2\), and \(I=-(\pi/2)\ln 2\). The mark scheme prints \(-(\pi/2)\ln 2\).

## Final answer

\[
-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\ln 2.
\]
