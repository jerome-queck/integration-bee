## Problem

\[
I=\int_0^{\pi/2}
\frac{x^2}{2\cdot3-x^2+
 \dfrac{2\cdot3x^2}{4\cdot5-x^2+
 \dfrac{4\cdot5x^2}{6\cdot7-x^2+\cdots}}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The continued fraction has an exact sine-series identity. To prove it without
numeric truncation, put \(d_n=2n(2n+1)\), and let \(A_N/B_N\) be the convergent
ending at denominator \(d_N-x^2\). Its numerator and denominator satisfy
\[
Y_N=(d_N-x^2)Y_{N-1}+d_{N-1}x^2Y_{N-2}\qquad(N\geq2),
\]
with \(A_0=0\), \(B_0=1\), \(A_1=x^2\), and \(B_1=6-x^2\).
Define
\[
D_N=(2N+1)!,\qquad S_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^kx^{2k}}{(2k+1)!}.
\]
The factorial relation \(D_N=d_ND_{N-1}\) and successive-term ratio
\(-x^2/d_N\) show that
\[
B_N=D_NS_N,\qquad A_N=D_N(1-S_N).
\]
They satisfy the recurrence and its initial values, which proves the finite
identity by induction.

For \(0\leq x\leq\pi/2\), the alternating series has decreasing absolute
terms, and
\(S_N\geq1-x^2/6\geq1-\pi^2/24>0\). Its factorial ratio tends to zero, so
\(S_N\to\sin x/x\). Therefore the continued fraction converges to
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1-S_N}{S_N}=x\csc x-1,
\]
with its continuous value \(0\) at \(x=0\). This proves the identity used by
the worked solution, including convergence of the original fraction.

Now differentiate \(x\ln\tan(x/2)\) and evaluate its zero endpoint limits:
\[
I=-\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\tan(x/2)\,dx-\frac\pi2
=-2\int_0^1\frac{\ln u}{1+u^2}\,du-\frac\pi2
=2G-\frac\pi2.
\]
The substitution is \(u=\tan(x/2)\). The geometric series gives the Catalan
value, with absolute summability after integration.

## Final answer

\[
\boxed{2G-\frac\pi2}.
\]
