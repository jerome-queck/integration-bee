## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\lfloor\cos\frac{\pi}{x}\Bigr\rfloor\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The cosine equals \(1\) only at isolated points, where the floor equals \(1\). Elsewhere the floor is \(0\) when \(\cos(\pi/x)\ge 0\) and \(-1\) when \(\cos(\pi/x)<0\). For each integer \(k\ge 1\), the cosine is nonnegative on
\[
\Biggl[\frac{2}{4k+1},\frac{2}{4k-1}\Biggr].
\]
Those lengths sum to
\[
2\sum_{k\ge 1}\Biggl(\frac{1}{4k-1}-\frac{1}{4k+1}\Biggr).
\]
The Leibniz series \(\pi/4=\sum_{n\ge 0}(-1)^{n}/(2n+1)\) rearranges as
\[
\frac{\pi}{4}=1-\sum_{k\ge 1}\Biggl(\frac{1}{4k-1}-\frac{1}{4k+1}\Biggr).
\]
The nonnegative set therefore has measure \(2(1-\pi/4)\), and the integral is
\[
-\bigl(1-2(1-\pi/4)\bigr)=1-\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(1-\pi/2\).

## Final answer

\[
1-\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
