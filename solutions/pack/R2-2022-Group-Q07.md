## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\ln x\,\ln(1+x^{2})\,dx.
\]

## Solution

On \([0,1]\),
\[
\ln(1+x^{2})=\sum_{n\ge 1}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{x^{2n}}{n},
\]
with Leibniz remainder at most \(1/n\). Also \(\int_{0}^{1}x^{2n}\ln x\,dx=-1/(2n+1)^{2}\), so
\[
I=-\sum_{n\ge 1}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{1}{n(2n+1)^{2}}.
\]
Partial fractions give
\[
\frac{1}{n(2n+1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{n}-\frac{2}{2n+1}-\frac{2}{(2n+1)^{2}}.
\]
The three alternating sums are \(\ln 2\), \(1-\pi/4\), and \(1-G\), where \(G=\sum_{k\ge 0}(-1)^{k}/(2k+1)^{2}\) is Catalan's constant. Therefore
\[
I=-\ln 2+2\Bigl(1-\frac{\pi}{4}\Bigr)+2(1-G)=-\ln 2+4-2G-\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(-\ln 2+4-2G-\pi/2\).

## Final answer

\[
-\ln 2+4-2G-\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
