## Problem

\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sin^4 x+\cos^4 x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The integrand has period \(\pi/2\) and is symmetric on each quarter, so the integral is eight times the integral from \(0\) to \(\pi/4\). There \(\sin^4 x+\cos^4 x=1-\frac12\sin^2 2x\), and
\[
8\int_0^{\pi/4}\frac{dx}{1-\frac12\sin^2 2x}=16\int_0^{\pi/4}\frac{\sec^2 2x}{2+\tan^2 2x}\,dx.
\]
The substitution \(u=\tan 2x\) turns the last integral into
\[
8\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{u^2+2}=2\sqrt{2}\,\pi.
\]
The answers sheet prints \(2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\).

## Final answer

\[
2\sqrt{2}\,\pi.
\]
