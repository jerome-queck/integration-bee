## Problem

\[\int\frac{1}{(x+2)\sqrt{(x+1)(x+3)}}\,dx.\]

## Solution

For \(x>-2\), the derivative of \(\operatorname{arcsec}(x+2)\) is \(1/\bigl((x+2)\sqrt{(x+2)^{2}-1}\bigr)\), and \((x+2)^{2}-1=(x+1)(x+3)\). On \(x<-3\) that expression has the wrong sign. The real antiderivative on both components is \(\operatorname{arcsec}|x+2|\). The tiebreaker sheet also prints \(2\arctan\sqrt{(x+1)/(x+3)}+C\). On \(x>0\) that arctangent has the same derivative as the printed arcsec.

## Final answer

The sheet prints \(\operatorname{arcsec}(x+2)+C\) for \(x>-2\). The catalog stores \(\operatorname{arcsec}|x+2|\).
