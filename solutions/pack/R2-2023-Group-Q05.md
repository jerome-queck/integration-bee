## Problem

\[
I=\int_0^\pi\int_0^{2\pi}
\sin y\,\exp\!\bigl(\sin y(\cos x-\sin x)\bigr)\,dx\,dy.
\]

## Reading the printed answer

The radical in the mark scheme covers only the numeral \(2\). The printed
answer is \(2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\sinh(\sqrt{2})\), with \(\pi\) outside
the radical. It does not print \(2\sqrt{2\pi}\sinh(\sqrt{2})\).

## Solution

Parametrize the unit sphere by
\[
\mathbf r(y,x)=(\sin y\cos x,\sin y\sin x,\cos y),
\qquad 0\leq y\leq\pi,\quad 0\leq x\leq2\pi.
\]
Its surface area element is \(dS=\sin y\,dx\,dy\). Apart from the
boundary seam and the poles, which have zero area, the parametrization
covers the sphere once. Set \(\mathbf a=(1,-1,0)\). Then
\[
I=\int_{S^2}e^{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf r}\,dS.
\]

The matrix
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
1/\sqrt2&0&1/\sqrt2\\
1/\sqrt2&0&-1/\sqrt2\\
0&1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]
satisfies \(Q^TQ=I_3\), \(\det Q=1\), and
\(Q^T\mathbf a=(0,0,\sqrt2)\). A rotation maps the unit sphere onto
itself and preserves surface area. Rotating the integration coordinates
therefore gives
\[
I=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi e^{\sqrt2\cos y}\sin y\,dy\,dx
 =2\pi\int_{-1}^1e^{\sqrt2 u}\,du.
\]
The substitution is \(u=\cos y\), so \(du=-\sin y\,dy\).
Consequently,
\[
I=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt2}\left(e^{\sqrt2}-e^{-\sqrt2}\right)
 =2\sqrt2\,\pi\sinh(\sqrt2).
\]

## Final answer

\[
\boxed{2\sqrt2\,\pi\sinh(\sqrt2)}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

The gate retains the original double integral as an explicit inner
`Integral` and an outer interval. Its sphere certificate checks the
original exponential coefficients, the squared surface Jacobian,
orthogonality and determinant of the rotation, the transformed exponent,
and the substitution endpoints. SymPy then evaluates the resulting
one-dimensional exponential integral exactly. No unevaluated integral is
accepted as an answer. Changes to either integration interval or to the
original integrand must be rejected, as must the misread radical and a
perturbed answer.
