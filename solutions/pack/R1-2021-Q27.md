## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\cos(ax)}{x^{2}+b^{2}}\,dx,\qquad a>0,\; b>0.
\]

## Solution

The solutions PDF sets \(I(a)\) equal to the integral and reaches the equation \(I''(a)=b^{2}I(a)\) with \(I(0)=\pi/(2b)\). It then states \(I'(0)=0\) and prints \(\pi e^{ab}/(2b)\). For \(a>0\) the split
\[
I'(a)=-\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)}{x}\,dx+b^{2}\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)}{x(x^{2}+b^{2})}\,dx
\]
has right-hand limit \(-\pi/2\) at \(a=0\), not \(0\). The solution of the same differential equation with that right derivative is \(\pi e^{-ab}/(2b)\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi e^{-ab}/(2b)\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi e^{-ab}}{2b}.
\]
