## Problem

\[
\int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+e^{2x}}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF rewrites the integrand as \(e^{-x}/\sqrt{1+e^{-2x}}\) and sets \(u=e^{-x}\):

\[
\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1+e^{2x}}}=-\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}=-\operatorname{arsinh}(e^{-x})+C.
\]

Let \(s=\sqrt{1+e^{2x}}\). The answers sheet prints \(\frac12\ln\bigl((s-1)/(s+1)\bigr)\). These agree. The identity \((s-1)(s+1)=e^{2x}\) gives

\[
\frac12\ln\frac{s-1}{s+1}=x-\ln(1+s),
\]

and

\[
-\operatorname{arsinh}(e^{-x})=-\ln\bigl(e^{-x}(1+s)\bigr)=x-\ln(1+s).
\]

## Final answer

\[
-\operatorname{arsinh}(e^{-x})+C=\frac12\ln\frac{\sqrt{1+e^{2x}}-1}{\sqrt{1+e^{2x}}+1}+C.
\]
