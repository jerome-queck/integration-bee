## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\Gamma(x)^{2}+\pi\csc(\pi x)}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Write \(c(x)=\pi\csc(\pi x)=\Gamma(x)\Gamma(1-x)\), and let
\[
A(x)=\Gamma(x)^{2}+c(x),\qquad B(x)=\Gamma(1-x)^{2}+c(x).
\]
The reflection \(x\mapsto 1-x\) swaps \(A\) and \(B\), so the integral \(I\) is unchanged. For positive \(g\) and \(h\) with \(c=gh\), \(A=g^{2}+c\) and \(B=h^{2}+c\),
\[
(A+B)c-AB=0,
\]
hence \((A+B)/(AB)=1/c\). Adding the integral to its reflection therefore gives
\[
2I=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi}\,dx=\frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}},
\]
so \(I=1/\pi^{2}\). The worked PDF reaches the same value after the reflection; the gamma lines in that write-up compress this cancellation. The mark scheme prints \(1/\pi^{2}\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{1}{\pi^{2}}.
\]
