## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos x}{\bigl(1+\sqrt{\sin 2x}\bigr)^2}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

King's substitution \(x\mapsto\pi/2-x\) replaces \(\cos x\) by \(\sin x\) and leaves the denominator unchanged, so the same value \(I\) is also the integral of the sine numerator. Adding the two copies,

\[
2I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin x+\cos x}{\bigl(1+\sqrt{\sin 2x}\bigr)^2}\,dx.
\]

Set \(u=\sin x-\cos x\). Then \(du=(\sin x+\cos x)\,dx\) and \(\sin 2x=1-u^2\), with limits \(-1\) to \(1\). The integrand is even, so

\[
I=\int_0^1\frac{du}{\bigl(1+\sqrt{1-u^2}\bigr)^2}.
\]

The worked PDF prints a minus sign in that denominator. The substitution produces a plus. The plus-form integral is the one evaluated next. The change of variables \(u=\sin\theta\) returns

\[
I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos\theta}{(1+\cos\theta)^2}\,d\theta
=\int_0^{\pi/2}\Biggl(\frac{1}{1+\cos\theta}-\frac{1}{(1+\cos\theta)^2}\Biggr)d\theta.
\]

The half-angle identities \(1+\cos\theta=2\cos^2(\theta/2)\) turn the integrand into

\[
\frac14\sec^2\frac\theta2-\frac14\sec^2\frac\theta2\tan^2\frac\theta2,
\]

whose antiderivative is \(\frac12\tan(\theta/2)-\frac16\tan^3(\theta/2)\). From \(0\) to \(\pi/2\) that equals \(\frac12-\frac16=\frac13\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac13.
\]
