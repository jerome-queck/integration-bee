## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{a^{2}-\sin^{2}x},\qquad a>1.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(t=\tan x\) runs from \(0\) to \(\infty\), and \(\sin^{2}x=t^{2}/(1+t^{2})\), \(dx=dt/(1+t^{2})\). The integrand becomes
\[
\frac{1}{a^{2}+(a^{2}-1)t^{2}}.
\]
An antiderivative is
\[
\frac{1}{a\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}\arctan\Biggl(\frac{t\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}{a}\Biggr).
\]
From \(t=0\) to \(t=\infty\) this equals \(\pi/(2a\sqrt{a^{2}-1})\). The mark scheme prints that value.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi}{2a\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}.
\]
