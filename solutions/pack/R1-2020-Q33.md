## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\arctan\Bigl(\frac{1}{x^2-x+1}\Bigr)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

On \([0,1]\) one has \(x(1-x)\le 1/4<1\), so the two-argument arctangent formula gives
\[
\arctan x+\arctan(1-x)=\arctan\Bigl(\frac{1}{x^2-x+1}\Bigr).
\]
The substitution \(x\mapsto 1-x\) shows that the two summands have the same integral. Integration by parts then yields
\[
2\int_0^1\arctan x\,dx=2\Bigl[x\arctan x-\frac12\ln(1+x^2)\Bigr]_0^1=\frac\pi2-\ln 2.
\]
The answers sheet prints \(\pi/2-\ln 2\).

\frac\pi2-\ln 2.
