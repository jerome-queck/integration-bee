## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(\cot x-1)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

On \((0,\pi/4)\), \(\cot x-1=(1-\tan x)/\tan x\), so
\[
\ln(\cot x-1)=\ln(1-\tan x)-\ln(\tan x).
\]
The reflection \(x\mapsto\pi/4-x\) replaces \(\ln(\tan x)\) by \(\ln((1-\tan x)/(1+\tan x))\). Subtracting leaves
\[
\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(\cot x-1)\,dx=\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(1+\tan x)\,dx.
\]
Call the right-hand side \(J\). The same reflection gives \(1+\tan(\pi/4-x)=2/(1+\tan x)\), hence \(J=(\pi/4)\ln 2-J\) and
\[
J=\frac\pi8\ln 2.
\]
The mark scheme prints \((\pi\ln 2)/8\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{8}\ln 2.
\]
