## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\infty}\lfloor x\rfloor e^{-x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Split at the integers, as in the worked PDF. On \([n,n+1]\) the floor is \(n\), for \(n\ge 1\), and the \(n=0\) piece vanishes:
\[
\int_n^{n+1}n e^{-x}\,dx=n\bigl(e^{-n}-e^{-(n+1)}\bigr).
\]
Rewrite the general term as a telescope plus a geometric term:
\[
n e^{-n}-(n+1)e^{-(n+1)}+e^{-(n+1)}.
\]
Summing from \(n=1\) leaves \(e^{-1}\) from the telescope and
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-(n+1)}=\frac{e^{-2}}{1-e^{-1}}.
\]
Adding these gives \(1/(e-1)\). The mark scheme prints \(1/(e-1)\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{1}{e-1}.
\]
