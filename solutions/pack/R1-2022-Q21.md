## Problem

\[
\int_{1/e}^\infty\frac{\sqrt{\ln x+1}}{x^2}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Set \(u=\ln x+1\), so \(x=e^{u-1}\) and \(dx=e^{u-1}\,du\). The limits go from \(0\) to \(\infty\), and

\[
\frac{\sqrt{\ln x+1}}{x^2}\,dx=\sqrt{u}\,e^{-u+1}\,du=e\sqrt{u}\,e^{-u}\,du.
\]

Therefore the integral is

\[
e\int_0^\infty u^{1/2}e^{-u}\,du=e\,\Gamma\Bigl(\frac32\Bigr)=e\cdot\frac12\Gamma\Bigl(\frac12\Bigr)=\frac{e\sqrt{\pi}}{2}.
\]

The worked PDF's second write-up reaches the same gamma integral after the substitution \(u=ex\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{e\sqrt{\pi}}{2}.
\]
