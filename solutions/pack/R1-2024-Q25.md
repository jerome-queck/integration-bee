## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\pi x)\ln\Gamma(x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Euler's reflection \(\Gamma(x)\Gamma(1-x)=\pi/\sin(\pi x)\) suggests the substitution in the solutions PDF. Replacing \(x\) by \(1-x\) leaves the sine factor fixed and replaces \(\Gamma(x)\) by \(\Gamma(1-x)\), so
\[
2I=\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\pi x)\ln\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi x)}\,dx=\frac{2\ln\pi}{\pi}-J,
\]
where \(J=\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\pi x)\ln(\sin(\pi x))\,dx\). The substitution \(u=\pi x\) and an integration by parts, with the improper boundary terms evaluated in that PDF, give \(J=2(\ln 2-1)/\pi\). Therefore
\[
I=\frac{\ln\pi-\ln 2+1}{\pi}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \((\ln\pi-\ln 2+1)/\pi\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\ln\pi-\ln 2+1}{\pi}.
\]
