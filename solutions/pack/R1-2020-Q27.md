## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\arctan x}{(1+x)\sqrt{x}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(u=1/x\) leaves the integral unchanged and replaces \(\arctan x\) by \(\arctan(1/x)\). For \(x>0\), \(\arctan x+\arctan(1/x)=\pi/2\), so twice the integral is

\[
\frac\pi2\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{(1+x)\sqrt{x}}.
\]

The further substitution \(\sqrt{x}=v\) turns the reduced integral into \(\int_0^\infty 2\,dv/(1+v^2)=\pi\). Multiplying by \(\pi/4\) gives the printed value. Equivalently, half of \(\pi/2\) times \(\pi\) is \(\pi^2/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\]
