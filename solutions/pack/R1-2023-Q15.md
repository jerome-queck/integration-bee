## Problem

\[
\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1+2x\ln 2}{x\sqrt{x\cdot 4^{x}-1}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF uses
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(x\cdot 4^{x}\bigr)=(1+2x\ln 2)\,4^{x}.
\]
Set \(u=x\cdot 4^{x}-1\). Then \(du=(1+2x\ln 2)\,4^{x}\,dx\) and \(u+1=x\cdot 4^{x}\), so the integrand is \(du/((u+1)\sqrt{u})\). As \(x\) runs from \(1\) to \(\infty\), \(u\) runs from \(3\) to \(\infty\). The further substitution \(u=t^{2}\) gives
\[
\int_{\sqrt{3}}^{\infty}\frac{2\,dt}{t^{2}+1}=2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{3}\right)=\frac{\pi}{3}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\pi/3\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{3}.
\]
