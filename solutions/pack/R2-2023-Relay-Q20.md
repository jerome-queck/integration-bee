## Problem

If
\[
\bigl(f(x)\bigr)^{2}=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{t f(t)}{1+t^{2}}\,dt,
\]
compute \(\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)\,dx\).

## Solution

Differentiating for \(x>0\) gives \(2f f'=x f/(1+x^{2})\). The zero function satisfies the integral equation, but the mark scheme does not print \(0\). Cancelling the positive factor, \(2f'=x/(1+x^{2})\) and \(f(0)=0\), so
\[
f(x)=\frac14\ln(1+x^{2})\qquad(x\ge 0).
\]
The same formula is even. Its integral is
\[
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac14\ln(1+x^{2})\,dx=\frac{\pi+\ln 4-4}{4}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \((\pi+\log 4-4)/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi+\log 4-4}{4}.
\]
