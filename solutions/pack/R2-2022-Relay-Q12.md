## Problem

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n+k}.
\]

## Solution

The sum is the difference of harmonic numbers \(H_{2n}-H_{n}\). At \(n=1\) both the harmonic difference and the sum equal \(1/2\). Passing from \(n\) to \(n+1\) adds
\[
\frac{1}{2n+1}+\frac{1}{2n+2}-\frac{1}{n+1}
\]
to each of them, so they agree for every positive integer \(n\).

For a direct limit proof, \(u\mapsto 1/(n+u)\) is decreasing. Bound each summand by the integrals on its adjacent unit intervals and sum:
\[
\int_1^{n+1}\frac{du}{n+u}
\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1{n+k}
\leq\int_0^n\frac{du}{n+u}.
\]
The bounds are \(\ln((2n+1)/(n+1))\) and \(\ln 2\). They both tend to \(\ln 2\), so the squeeze theorem gives the limit. The mark scheme prints \(\ln 2\).

## Final answer

\[
\ln 2.
\]
