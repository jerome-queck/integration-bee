## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln(x+1/x)}{x+1/x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Multiply the numerator and the denominator by \(x\):
\[
\frac{\ln(x+1/x)}{x+1/x}=\frac{x\ln(x^{2}+1)-x\ln x}{x^{2}+1}.
\]
The worked PDF splits the integral into these two pieces. For the first, \(w=x^{2}+1\) gives
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x\ln(x^{2}+1)}{x^{2}+1}\,dx=\frac12\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\ln w}{w}\,dw=\frac{(\ln 2)^{2}}{4}.
\]
For the second, expand \(1/(x^{2}+1)=\sum_{n\ge 0}(-1)^{n}x^{2n}\) on \([0,1)\):
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x\ln x}{x^{2}+1}\,dx=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\int_{0}^{1}x^{2n+1}\ln x\,dx=-\frac14\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{48}.
\]
Subtracting this piece from the first produces \((\ln 2)^{2}/4+\pi^{2}/48\). The series display in the worked PDF is compressed; the mark scheme prints \(\pi^{2}/48+(1/4)(\ln 2)^{2}\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{(\ln 2)^{2}}{4}+\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{48}.
\]
