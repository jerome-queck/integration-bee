## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-t^2}\cos(2xt)\,dt.
\]

## Solution

Write \(I(x)\) for the integral. Differentiating under the integral sign and integrating by parts,

\[
I'(x)=\int_0^\infty -2t e^{-t^2}\sin(2xt)\,dt=-2x\,I(x).
\]

The worked PDF's first display of \(I'\) omits the minus from \(\partial/\partial x\cos(2xt)\). The integration by parts it then prints reaches the differential equation \(I'=-2x I\), which is the equation solved here. Thus \(I(x)=Ce^{-x^2}\). The constant is the Gaussian integral

\[
C=I(0)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t^2}\,dt=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}.
\]

The mark scheme prints \(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}e^{-x^2}\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}e^{-x^2}.
\]
