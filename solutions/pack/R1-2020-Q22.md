## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^3 x}{\sin x+\cos x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The reflection \(x\mapsto\pi/2-x\) replaces the integrand by \(\cos^3 x/(\sin x+\cos x)\) and leaves the integral unchanged. Averaging the two copies gives
\[
\frac12\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^3 x+\cos^3 x}{\sin x+\cos x}\,dx=\frac12\int_0^{\pi/2}\bigl(1-\sin x\cos x\bigr)\,dx.
\]
Then \(\sin x\cos x=\sin(2x)/2\), so the value is
\[
\frac12\int_0^{\pi/2}\Bigl(1-\frac12\sin 2x\Bigr)\,dx=\frac\pi4-\frac14.
\]
The answers sheet prints \((\pi-1)/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi-1}{4}.
\]
