## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{x\sin x}{3+\cos^{2}x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(x\mapsto\pi-x\) replaces the factor \(x\) by \(\pi-x\) and leaves \(\sin x\) and \(\cos^{2}x\) unchanged. Adding the two copies,
\[
2I=\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\sin x}{3+\cos^{2}x}\,dx.
\]
The further substitution \(u=\cos x\) gives
\[
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{du}{3+u^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}.
\]
Hence \(I=\pi^{2}/(6\sqrt{3})\), which is the same number as \(\sqrt{3}\,\pi^{2}/18\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi^{2}/(6\sqrt{3})\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\sqrt{3}}.
\]
