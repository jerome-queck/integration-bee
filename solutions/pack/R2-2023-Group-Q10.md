## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\arctan(1+\cos x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Let \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2\), so \(\varphi-1/\varphi=1\). Since \(1+\cos x=2\cos^{2}(x/2)\),

\[
I=\operatorname{Im}\int_{0}^{\pi}\log\bigl(1+i(1+\cos x)\bigr)\,dx=\operatorname{Im}\int_{0}^{\pi}\log(b+c\cos x)\,dx
\]

with \(b=1+i\) and \(c=i\). For real \(b>c>0\),

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\log(b+c\cos x)\,dx=\pi\log\frac{b+\sqrt{b^{2}-c^{2}}}{2}.
\]

Differentiating in \(b\) produces \(\pi/\sqrt{b^{2}-c^{2}}\) on the right. On the left, \(t=\tan(x/2)\) sends \((0,\pi)\) to \((0,\infty)\) and turns the integrand into \(2/((b+c)+(b-c)t^{2})\), whose integral is the same quantity. As \(b\to\infty\), both sides differ from \(\pi\log b\) by a term that tends to \(0\), so the constant matches.

Both sides are holomorphic on the connected domain \(\operatorname{Re}(b+c)>0\), \(\operatorname{Re}(b-c)>0\). The square root has positive real part there, fixing its branch. The identity continues to \(b=1+i\) and \(c=i\) because \(\operatorname{Re}(b+c\cos x)=1>0\), so the logarithm stays off its branch cut. Here \(b^{2}-c^{2}=1+2i\), and the square root with positive real part is \(\sqrt{\varphi}+i/\sqrt{\varphi}\):

\[
\bigl(\sqrt{\varphi}+i/\sqrt{\varphi}\bigr)^{2}=\varphi-1/\varphi+2i=1+2i.
\]

The argument of \(b+\sqrt{b^{2}-c^{2}}\) is \(\arctan(1/\sqrt{\varphi})=\operatorname{acot}\sqrt{\varphi}\). Dividing by \(2\) does not change the argument, so \(I=\pi\operatorname{acot}\sqrt{\varphi}\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi\cot^{-1}(\sqrt{\varphi})\).

## Final answer

\[
\pi\cot^{-1}\sqrt{\varphi},\qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}.
\]
