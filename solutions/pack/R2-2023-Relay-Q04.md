## Problem

Suppose \(4f''(x)+4f'(x)+f(x)=1/(e^{x}-1)\) and \(e^{x/2}f(x)\to 0\) as \(x\to\infty\). Evaluate

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The differential operator is \((2D+1)^{2}\). Set \(g=(2D+1)f\), so \(2g'+g=1/(e^{x}-1)\). The integrating factor \(e^{x/2}\) gives

\[
\bigl(e^{x/2}g\bigr)'=\frac12\frac{e^{x/2}}{e^{x}-1}=\frac{d}{dx}\Bigl(\frac12\log\tanh\frac{x}{4}\Bigr)
\]

for \(x>0\), because \(\tanh(x/4)=(e^{x/2}-1)/(e^{x/2}+1)\). Thus \(e^{x/2}g=\frac12\log\tanh(x/4)+C\), and

\[
\bigl(e^{x/2}f\bigr)'=\frac14\log\tanh\frac{x}{4}+\frac{C}{2}.
\]

The logarithm decays exponentially at infinity, so its integral from \(x\) to \(\infty\) converges. A nonzero \(C\) makes that integral diverge, which would stop \(e^{x/2}f\) from tending to \(0\). The same limit kills the homogeneous solutions \((A+Bx)e^{-x/2}\). Hence

\[
f(x)=e^{-x/2}\Bigl(-\frac14\int_{x}^{\infty}\log\tanh\frac{t}{4}\,dt\Bigr).
\]

The weight \(|\log\tanh(t/4)|\,(1-e^{-t/2})\) is integrable on \((0,\infty)\), so the order of integration may be switched:

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x/2}\Bigl(-\frac14\int_{x}^{\infty}\log\tanh\frac{t}{4}\,dt\Bigr)dx=-\frac12\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\tanh\frac{t}{4}\,(1-e^{-t/2})\,dt.
\]

The substitution \(u=t/4\) produces \(-2\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(\tanh u)\,(1-e^{-2u})\,du\). For \(u>0\),

\[
\log\tanh u=\log(1-e^{-2u})-\log(1+e^{-2u})=-2\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{e^{-2(2m+1)u}}{2m+1},
\]

the even powers cancelling. Each term integrates to

\[
\frac{2}{(2m+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(2m+1)(m+1)}.
\]

The odd-square series is \(\pi^{2}/8\). Partial fractions and the harmonic limit

\[
\sum_{m=0}^{N}\Bigl(\frac{2}{2m+1}-\frac{1}{m+1}\Bigr)=2H_{2N+1}-H_{N}-H_{N+1}\to 2\log 2
\]

give \(\pi^{2}/4-2\log 2\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi^{2}/4-2\ln 2\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}-2\ln 2.
\]
