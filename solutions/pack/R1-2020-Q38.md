## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos x}{2-\sin 2x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(x\mapsto\pi/2-x\) replaces \(\cos x\) by \(\sin x\) and leaves \(\sin 2x\) fixed, so the integral \(I\) also equals \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x/(2-\sin 2x)\,dx\). Adding the two copies,
\[
2I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin x+\cos x}{2-\sin 2x}\,dx.
\]
Set \(u=\sin x-\cos x\). Then \(du=(\cos x+\sin x)\,dx\) and \(u^{2}=1-\sin 2x\), so \(2-\sin 2x=1+u^{2}\). The endpoints are \(u(0)=-1\) and \(u(\pi/2)=1\), and
\[
2I=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{du}{1+u^{2}}=\frac\pi2.
\]
Thus \(I=\pi/4\). The answers sheet prints \(\pi/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{4}.
\]
