## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin(x^{2})\,dx.
\]

## Solution

This is the Fresnel integral. The Gaussian evaluation
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-zx^{2}}\,dx=\sqrt{\pi/z}
\]
for \(\operatorname{Re}z>0\), continued to \(z=-i\), has imaginary part
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sin(x^{2})\,dx=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}.
\]
The integrand is even, so the integral from \(0\) to \(\infty\) is half of that:
\[
\frac12\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}.
\]
The mark scheme prints this value.

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}.
\]
