## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\arcsin(x^2)+\arcsin\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Split the integral and substitute \(u=\sqrt{x}\) in the second term only, so \(dx=2u\,du\):

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\arcsin(x^2)}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx+\int_0^1\frac{2u\arcsin u}{\sqrt{1-u^4}}\,du.
\]

The second integrand is the derivative of a product, up to the first integrand. Integration by parts gives

\[
\Bigl[\arcsin u\cdot\arcsin(u^2)\Bigr]_0^1=\arcsin(1)\arcsin(1)=\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\]

The remaining integrals cancel. The mark scheme prints \(\pi^2/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\]
