## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\arctan^{2}x\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Set \(x=\tan\theta\). Then \(dx=\sec^{2}\theta\,d\theta\) and the range is \([0,\pi/4]\), so the integral is \(\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\theta^{2}\sec^{2}\theta\,d\theta\). Differentiating \(\theta^{2}\tan\theta\) isolates that integrand:
\[
\theta^{2}\sec^{2}\theta=\frac{d}{d\theta}(\theta^{2}\tan\theta)-2\theta\tan\theta.
\]
The boundary term at \(\pi/4\) is \((\pi/4)^{2}\). A second integration by parts uses
\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}(\theta\ln\cos\theta)=\ln\cos\theta-\theta\tan\theta,
\]
and \(\theta\ln\cos\theta\) vanishes at both endpoints, with value \(-(\pi/8)\ln 2\) at \(\pi/4\). Thus
\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\theta\tan\theta\,d\theta=\frac{\pi}{8}\ln 2+\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\ln\cos\theta\,d\theta.
\]
The Fourier series \(\ln(2\sin\phi)=-\sum_{k\ge 1}\cos(2k\phi)/k\) comes from the Abel sum \(\sum r^{k}\sin(k\phi)\to\cot(\phi/2)/2\). The shift \(\phi=\pi/2-\theta\) gives
\[
\ln\cos\theta=-\ln 2+\sum_{k\ge 1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\cos(2k\theta)}{k}.
\]
Integrating termwise over \([0,\pi/4]\) leaves the odd terms, and that series is half of Catalan's constant \(G\). Therefore
\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\ln\cos\theta\,d\theta=-\frac{\pi}{4}\ln 2+\frac{G}{2},
\]
and the original integral is \(\pi^{2}/16+(\pi\ln 2)/4-G\). The mark scheme prints that value.

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{16}+\dfrac{\pi\ln 2}{4}-G.
\]
