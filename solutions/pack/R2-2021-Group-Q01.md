## Problem

\[
I=\int_0^1\arctan^2x\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Integration by parts and then \(x=\tan\theta\) give
\[
I=\frac{\pi^2}{16}-2\int_0^1\frac{x\arctan x}{1+x^2}\,dx
=\frac{\pi^2}{16}-2\int_0^{\pi/4}\theta\tan\theta\,d\theta.
\]
Since
\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}\bigl(\theta\ln\cos\theta\bigr)
=\ln\cos\theta-\theta\tan\theta,
\]
the second integral is
\[
\int_0^{\pi/4}\theta\tan\theta\,d\theta
=\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\cos\theta\,d\theta+\frac\pi8\ln2.
\]
The endpoint product is zero at \(0\) and \(-\pi\ln2/8\) at \(\pi/4\).

Write \(S=\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\sin\theta\,d\theta\) and
\(C=\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\cos\theta\,d\theta\). The double-angle identity and the
usual reflected log-sine integral give
\[
S+C=-\frac\pi2\ln2.
\]
Also, \(u=\tan\theta\) and the geometric series give
\[
S-C=\int_0^1\frac{\ln u}{1+u^2}\,du
=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2}=-G.
\]
The integrated absolute terms sum to \(\sum(2k+1)^{-2}<\infty\), so the
termwise integration is justified. Consequently
\(C=-\pi\ln2/4+G/2\), and substitution yields the printed final.

## Final answer

\[
\boxed{\frac{\pi^2}{16}+\frac\pi4\ln2-G}.
\]
