## Problem

\[
\int_0^{1}\frac{\arctan x}{1+x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Integrate by parts with \(u=\arctan x\) and \(dv=dx/(1+x)\). The boundary term is \((\pi/4)\ln 2\), and
\[
\int_0^{1}\frac{\arctan x}{1+x}\,dx=\frac\pi4\ln 2-\int_0^{1}\frac{\ln(1+x)}{1+x^{2}}\,dx.
\]
The substitution \(x=\tan\theta\) turns the remaining integral into
\[
J=\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(1+\tan\theta)\,d\theta.
\]
The reflection \(\theta\mapsto\pi/4-\theta\) uses \(1+\tan(\pi/4-\theta)=2/(1+\tan\theta)\), so \(J=(\pi/4)\ln 2-J\) and \(J=(\pi/8)\ln 2\). Therefore the original integral is
\[
\frac\pi4\ln 2-\frac\pi8\ln 2=\frac\pi8\ln 2.
\]
The mark scheme prints \((\pi\ln 2)/8\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{8}\ln 2.
\]
