## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sqrt{\cos\theta}}{\bigl(\sqrt{\cos\theta}+\sqrt{\sin\theta}\bigr)^5}\,d\theta.
\]

## Solution

Divide numerator and denominator by \(\cos^{5/2}\theta\):

\[
\frac{\sqrt{\cos\theta}}{\bigl(\sqrt{\cos\theta}+\sqrt{\sin\theta}\bigr)^5}
=\frac{\sec^2\theta}{\bigl(1+\sqrt{\tan\theta}\bigr)^5}.
\]

The substitution \(u^2=\tan\theta\) has \(2u\,du=\sec^2\theta\,d\theta\), with limits \(0\) to \(\infty\). The integral becomes

\[
2\int_0^\infty\frac{u}{(1+u)^5}\,du=2\int_0^\infty\Bigl((1+u)^{-4}-(1+u)^{-5}\Bigr)du.
\]

The antiderivative is \(-\frac23(1+u)^{-3}+\frac12(1+u)^{-4}\). At the endpoints that is \(\frac23-\frac12=\frac16\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac16.
\]
