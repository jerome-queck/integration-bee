## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\ln(x^2+1)}{x^2+1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(x=\tan\theta\) uses \(1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta\), so the integral becomes
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sec^2\theta)\,d\theta=-2\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\cos\theta)\,d\theta.
\]
The worked PDF quotes the standard log-cosine evaluation of that integral, \(\pi\ln 2\). The answers sheet prints \(\pi\ln 2\).

## Final answer

\[
\pi\ln 2.
\]
