## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{4}+4}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF reduces the integral, by \(u=\sqrt{2}\,x\) and then a fourth-power substitution, to a beta value:
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^{4}+4}=\frac{1}{8\sqrt{2}}\,B\left(\frac14,\frac34\right).
\]
Euler's reflection gives
\[
B\left(\frac14,\frac34\right)=\Gamma\left(\frac14\right)\Gamma\left(\frac34\right)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi/4)}=\pi\sqrt{2},
\]
and \(\pi\sqrt{2}/(8\sqrt{2})=\pi/8\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi/8\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{8}.
\]
