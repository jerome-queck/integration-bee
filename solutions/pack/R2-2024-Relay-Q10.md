## Problem

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\theta r}-1-i\theta\sin r}{|r|^{1+\alpha}}\,dr,
\]
for \(\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb{R}\).

## Solution

The imaginary part of the numerator is odd and the denominator is even. Near the origin the numerator is \(O(r^{3})\) and at infinity it is bounded, so the integral converges absolutely on the stated range of \(\alpha\). The imaginary part integrates to \(0\).

The real part is
\[
-2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(\theta r)}{r^{1+\alpha}}\,dr.
\]
It is even in \(\theta\). For \(\theta>0\) the scaling \(r=u/\theta\) factors out \(|\theta|^{\alpha}\) and leaves
\[
-2|\theta|^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos u}{u^{1+\alpha}}\,du.
\]
The gamma representation \(u^{-1-\alpha}=\Gamma(\alpha+1)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\alpha}e^{-us}\,ds\) and the Laplace transform
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos u)e^{-su}\,du=\frac{1}{s(s^{2}+1)}
\]
reduce the \(u\)-integral to
\[
\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{\alpha-1}}{1+s^{2}}\,ds=\frac{\pi}{2\Gamma(\alpha+1)\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}.
\]
The reflection formula \(\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin(\pi z)\) at \(z=-\alpha\) is
\[
\Gamma(-\alpha)\Gamma(1+\alpha)=-\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi\alpha)}.
\]
Combined with \(\sin(\pi\alpha)=2\sin(\pi\alpha/2)\cos(\pi\alpha/2)\), the constant in front of \(|\theta|^{\alpha}\) is exactly \(2\Gamma(-\alpha)\cos(\pi\alpha/2)\). The solutions sheet quotes the same closed form from the Lévy–Khintchine formula for a symmetric stable law; the gamma calculation above evaluates the integral directly.

## Final answer

\[
2\Gamma(-\alpha)\cos\Bigl(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\Bigr)\,|\theta|^{\alpha}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

For \(\alpha>0\), SymPy simplifies \(\Gamma(-\alpha)\Gamma(1+\alpha)\sin(\pi\alpha)+\pi\) to \(0\). For \(s>0\), it evaluates \(\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos u)e^{-su}\,du=1/(s(s^{2}+1))\).
