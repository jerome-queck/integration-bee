## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{e^{x}-1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Multiply numerator and denominator by \(e^{-x}\):
\[
\frac{\sin x}{e^{x}-1}=\frac{e^{-x}\sin x}{1-e^{-x}}=\sum_{n\ge 1}e^{-nx}\sin x,
\]
the geometric series being valid for \(x>0\). Term by term,
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nx}\sin x\,dx=\frac{1}{n^{2}+1},
\]
which is the Laplace transform of \(\sin x\). The resulting series is the value at \(z=i\) of the partial-fraction expansion of \(\pi\cot(\pi z)/z\). Its closed form is
\[
\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}=\frac{\pi}{2}\coth\pi-\frac12.
\]

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{2}\coth\pi-\dfrac{1}{2}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy sums \(\sum_{n\ge 1}1/(n^{2}+1)\) to \((\pi/2)\coth\pi-1/2\).
