## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{e^{x}-1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Multiply numerator and denominator by \(e^{-x}\):
\[
\frac{\sin x}{e^{x}-1}=\frac{e^{-x}\sin x}{1-e^{-x}}=\sum_{n\ge 1}e^{-nx}\sin x,
\]
the geometric series being valid for \(x>0\). The interchange is absolutely justified: for every \(n\geq1\),
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-nx}|\sin x|\,dx
\leq\int_0^\infty xe^{-nx}\,dx=\frac1{n^2},
\qquad \sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}<\infty.
\]
Term by term,
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nx}\sin x\,dx=\frac{1}{n^{2}+1},
\]
which is the Laplace transform of \(\sin x\). Evaluate the partial-fraction expansion of \(\pi\cot(\pi z)/z\) at \(z=i\), isolate the zero term, and pair the positive and negative indices to obtain
\[
\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}=\frac{\pi}{2}\coth\pi-\frac12.
\]

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{2}\coth\pi-\dfrac{1}{2}.
\]
