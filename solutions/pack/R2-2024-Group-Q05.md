## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\ln\bigl(\sqrt{1+\sqrt{x}}+\sqrt{1-\sqrt{x}}\bigr)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The square of the argument is \(2+2\sqrt{1-x}\), so the integral is
\[
\frac12\int_{0}^{1}\ln\bigl(2+2\sqrt{1-x}\bigr)\,dx=\frac{\ln 2}{2}+\frac12\int_{0}^{1}\ln\bigl(1+\sqrt{1-x}\bigr)\,dx.
\]
Put \(u=\sqrt{1-x}\), so \(dx=-2u\,du\) and the limits swap from \(1\) to \(0\):
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\ln\bigl(1+\sqrt{1-x}\bigr)\,dx=2\int_{0}^{1}u\ln(1+u)\,du.
\]
The antiderivative
\[
\frac{u^{2}}{2}\ln(1+u)-\frac{u^{2}}{4}+\frac{u}{2}-\frac12\ln(1+u)
\]
has derivative \(u\ln(1+u)\). From \(0\) to \(1\) it equals \(1/4\), and twice that is \(1/2\). Adding the logarithm term gives
\[
\frac{\ln 2}{2}+\frac14.
\]

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\ln 2}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy differentiates \(\tfrac12 u^{2}\ln(1+u)-u^{2}/4+u/2-\tfrac12\ln(1+u)\) back to \(u\ln(1+u)\), and evaluates \(\int_{0}^{1}u\ln(1+u)\,du=1/4\).
