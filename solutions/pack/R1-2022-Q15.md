## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos^2 x\,(1+\cos x)}{(1+\cos x+\sin x)^2}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF uses the denominator identity

\[
(1+\cos x+\sin x)^2=2(1+\cos x)(1+\sin x).
\]

On \([0,\pi/2]\) one has \(1+\cos x>0\), so that factor cancels and the integral becomes

\[
\frac12\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos^2 x}{1+\sin x}\,dx
=\frac12\int_0^{\pi/2}(1-\sin x)\,dx,
\]

where the second step multiplies numerator and denominator by \(1-\sin x\). The remaining integral is

\[
\frac12\Bigl[x+\cos x\Bigr]_0^{\pi/2}=\frac12\Bigl(\frac\pi2-1\Bigr)=\frac{\pi-2}{4}.
\]

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi-2}{4}.
\]
