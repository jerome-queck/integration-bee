## Problem

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x+x\sin x}{x^{2}+\cos^{2}x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Write
\[
f(x)=\frac{\cos x+x\sin x}{x^{2}+\cos^{2}x}.
\]
Dividing the numerator and the denominator by \(\cos^{2}x\) produces
\[
f(x)=\frac{\sec x+x\sec x\tan x}{u},\qquad u=1+x^{2}\sec^{2}x.
\]
The chain rule gives \(u'=2x\sec x\,(\sec x+x\sec x\tan x)\), and
\[
x\sec x=\operatorname{sign}(x\cos x)\sqrt{u-1},
\]
so
\[
f(x)\,dx=\frac{du}{2u\sqrt{u-1}\,\operatorname{sign}(x\cos x)}.
\]
On each interval \(\bigl((n+\tfrac12)\pi,(n+\tfrac32)\pi\bigr)\) the factors \(x\) and \(\cos x\) do not change sign, while \(u\to\infty\) at both ends. The integral of \(du/(u\sqrt{u-1})\) depends only on the endpoints in \(u\), so each of those intervals contributes \(0\). The same cancellation holds for the matching intervals on the negative axis.

The remaining interval is \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\). On \((0,\pi/2)\) one has \(\operatorname{sign}(x\cos x)=1\) and \(u\) runs from \(1\) to \(\infty\), contributing
\[
\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{du}{2u\sqrt{u-1}}=\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
On \((-\pi/2,0)\) the sign is \(-1\) and \(u\) runs from \(\infty\) down to \(1\), which contributes another \(\pi/2\). The solutions sheet reaches \(\pi\) by the same substitution; the sign of \(\cos x\) has to be kept constant between consecutive zeros of \(\cos\).

## Final answer

\[
\pi.
\]
