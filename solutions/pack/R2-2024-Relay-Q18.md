## Problem

\[
\int_{-1}^{1}\ln\Bigl(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\Bigr)\ln(1+e^{x})\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The reflection \(x\mapsto -x\) replaces the integral \(I\) by
\[
\int_{-1}^{1}\ln\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\Bigr)\ln(1+e^{-x})\,dx=-\int_{-1}^{1}\ln\Bigl(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\Bigr)\bigl(\ln(1+e^{x})-x\bigr)\,dx.
\]
Adding this copy to the original cancels the \(\ln(1+e^{x})\) terms and leaves
\[
2I=\int_{-1}^{1}x\ln\Bigl(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\Bigr)\,dx.
\]
An antiderivative is
\[
\frac12\Biggl((x^{2}-1)\ln\Bigl(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\Bigr)+2x\Biggr).
\]
The factor \(x^{2}-1\) kills the logarithm at both endpoints, and the remaining \(x\) terms differ by \(2\). Thus \(2I=2\) and \(I=1\).

## Final answer

\[
1.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy differentiates \(\tfrac12\bigl((x^{2}-1)\ln((1+x)/(1-x))+2x\bigr)\) back to \(x\ln((1+x)/(1-x))\). The limits of that antiderivative at \(1\) and at \(-1\) differ by \(2\).
