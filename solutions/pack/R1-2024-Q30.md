## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\operatorname{acosh}(\sin x+\cos x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

\(\sin x+\cos x=\sqrt{2}\cos(x-\pi/4)\), so the integral is

\[
2\int_0^{\pi/4}\operatorname{acosh}(\sqrt{2}\cos\phi)\,d\phi.
\]

The worked PDF introduces

\[
I(t)=\int_0^{\operatorname{arcsec} t}\operatorname{acosh}(t\cos\phi)\,d\phi,\qquad t\ge 1,
\]

with \(I(1)=0\). Differentiating under the integral sign, the endpoint contribution vanishes, and \(u=t\sin\phi\) gives

\[
I'(t)=\int_0^{\sqrt{t^2-1}}\frac{du}{t\sqrt{t^2-1-u^2}}=\frac{\pi}{2t}.
\]

Thus \(I(t)=(\pi/2)\ln t\). The original integral is \(2I(\sqrt{2})=(\pi/2)\ln 2\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac\pi2\ln 2.
\]
