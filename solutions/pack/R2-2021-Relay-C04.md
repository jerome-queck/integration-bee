## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos x}{c-\sin 2x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Shuttle C3 passes \(c=2\). Shift \(x=\pi/4-u\). The odd part in \(\sin u\) drops out, and the integral equals
\[
\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\frac{\cos u}{2-\cos 2u}\,du.
\]
Then \(2-\cos 2u=1+2\sin^{2}u\). Set \(w=\sin u\):
\[
\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2}/2}\frac{dw}{1+2w^{2}}=\arctan 1=\frac{\pi}{4}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\pi/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{4}.
\]
