## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{x}{\sin x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The identity \(1/\sin x=2i e^{-ix}/(1-e^{-2ix})\) is correct on \((0,\pi)\), but \(|e^{-2ix}|=1\), so the geometric series does not converge there and cannot be integrated term by term as written. For \(0<r<1\),

\[
\frac{2i e^{-ix}}{1-r e^{-2ix}}=2i\sum_{n\ge 0} r^n e^{-i(2n+1)x}.
\]

Integrate \(x\) times this sum from \(0\) to \(\pi/2\), integrate by parts, and let \(r\uparrow 1\). On each compact subinterval of \((0,\pi/2)\) the convergence is uniform, and near the endpoints \(x\csc x\) is integrable, so the limit passes inside the integral. The resulting series are absolutely convergent. Integration by parts produces
\[
\pi i\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}+2\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n+1)^{2}}-2i\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{2}}.
\]
Those series are \(\pi/4\), Catalan's constant \(G\), and \(\pi^{2}/8\). The imaginary parts \(\pi i\cdot\pi/4-2i\cdot\pi^{2}/8\) cancel, leaving \(2G\). The mark scheme prints \(2G\).

## Final answer

\[
2G.
\]
