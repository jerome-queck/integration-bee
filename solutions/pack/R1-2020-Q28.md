## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\ln x\,\ln(1-x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Expand \(\ln(1-x)=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty x^n/n\) on \([0,1)\). The worked PDF integrates term by term:

\[
\int_0^1\ln x\,\ln(1-x)\,dx=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1n\int_0^1(-\ln x)\,x^n\,dx=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n(n+1)^2}.
\]

Partial fractions give \(\dfrac{1}{n(n+1)^2}=\dfrac1n-\dfrac{1}{n+1}-\dfrac{1}{(n+1)^2}\). The first two terms telescope to \(1\), and

\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{(n+1)^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}-1,
\]

so the series equals \(1-(\pi^2/6-1)=2-\pi^2/6\).

## Final answer

\[
2-\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\]
