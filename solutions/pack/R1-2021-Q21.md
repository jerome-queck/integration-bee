## Problem

\[
\int_0^a\frac{x}{\cos x\cos(a-x)}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Let \(I\) be the integral. The substitution \(x\mapsto a-x\) gives a second copy with numerator \(a-x\). Adding the copies produces
\[
I=\frac{a}{2}\int_0^a\frac{dx}{\cos x\cos(a-x)}.
\]
The angle sum \(\sin a=\sin((a-x)+x)\) rewrites the integrand as \((\tan(a-x)+\tan x)/\sin a\). Those two tangents have the same integral, so
\[
I=\frac{a}{\sin a}\int_0^a\tan x\,dx=\frac{a\ln|\sec a|}{\sin a}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(a\ln|\sec a|/\sin a\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{a\ln|\sec a|}{\sin a}.
\]
