## Problem

\[
\int\frac{\ln(e^{x}-1)-x}{e^{x}-1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

On \(x>0\), where the logarithm in the problem is real,
\[
\ln(e^{x}-1)-x=\ln(1-e^{-x}),
\]
and dividing numerator and denominator by \(e^{x}\) rewrites the integrand as
\[
\frac{e^{-x}\ln(1-e^{-x})}{1-e^{-x}}.
\]
The substitution \(u=1-e^{-x}\) gives \(\int(\ln u)/u\,du=(\ln u)^{2}/2\). Substituting back,
\[
\frac{(\ln(1-e^{-x}))^{2}}{2}=\frac{(x-\ln(e^{x}-1))^{2}}{2}.
\]
The two antiderivatives printed on the mark scheme are the same function on \(x>0\), not merely two functions that differ by a constant.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{(\ln(1-e^{-x}))^{2}}{2}+C
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac{(x-\ln(e^{x}-1))^{2}}{2}+C.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy differentiates both printed antiderivatives back to \((\ln(e^{x}-1)-x)/(e^{x}-1)\) and simplifies the difference of those derivatives to \(0\).
