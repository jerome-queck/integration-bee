## Problem

Find the volume of the unit ball in four dimensions.

## Solution

The unit ball is \(w^{2}+x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1\). Slicing in \(z\) leaves a three-dimensional ball of radius \(\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\), whose volume is \(\tfrac43\pi r^{3}\). Thus
\[
V=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{4}{3}\pi(1-z^{2})^{3/2}\,dz=\frac{8}{3}\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{4}\theta\,d\theta,
\]
after \(z=\sin\theta\). Wallis' integral \(\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{4}\theta\,d\theta=3\pi/16\), so
\[
V=\frac{8}{3}\pi\cdot\frac{3\pi}{16}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\pi^{2}/2\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}.
\]
