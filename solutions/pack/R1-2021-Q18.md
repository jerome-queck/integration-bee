## Problem

\[
\int\sqrt{\frac1x-1}\,dx,\qquad 0<x<1.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF sets \(u=\sqrt{x}\), then \(u=\sin\theta\):

\[
\int\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{x}}\,dx=2\int\sqrt{1-u^2}\,du=\theta+\frac12\sin 2\theta+C
=\arcsin\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{x(1-x)}+C.
\]

The mark scheme prints \(\sqrt{x(1-x)}-\arctan\sqrt{1/x-1}+C\). The two antiderivatives differ by the constant \(\pi/2\). Both differentiate back to the integrand.

## Final answer

\[
\arcsin\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{x(1-x)}+C.
\]
