## Problem

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{2}(1+x^{4})^{n}\,dx\Biggr)^{1/n}.
\]

## Solution

On \([0,2]\) the continuous function \(1+x^{4}\) attains its maximum at the right endpoint, and that maximum is \(1+16=17\). The integral is at most \(2\cdot 17^{n}\), so the root is at most \(17\cdot 2^{1/n}\), which tends to \(17\).

For a lower bound, restrict to \([2-\delta,2]\). There \(1+x^{4}\ge 1+(2-\delta)^{4}\), and the root of the integral is at least \(\delta^{1/n}\) times that number. Letting \(n\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow 0\) shows that the liminf is at least \(17\). The limit is \(17\).

## Final answer

\[
17.
\]
