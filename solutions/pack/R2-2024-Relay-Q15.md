## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sin^{2}(\pi x/2)}{\sqrt{x-x^{2}}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The reflection \(x\mapsto 1-x\) replaces \(\sin(\pi x/2)\) by \(\cos(\pi x/2)\) and leaves the square root unchanged. Adding the integral to its reflection replaces \(\sin^{2}\) by \(1\):
\[
2I=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x-x^{2}}}.
\]
Completing the square, \(x-x^{2}=1/4-(x-1/2)^{2}\). The substitution \(y=x-1/2\) gives
\[
\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\frac{dy}{\sqrt{1/4-y^{2}}}=\pi,
\]
so \(I=\pi/2\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{2}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy evaluates \(\int_{0}^{1}dx/\sqrt{x-x^{2}}=\pi\). Half of that value is \(\pi/2\).
