## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\arctan^2 x}{x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Integration by parts removes the boundary term, because \(x\ln x\to 0\) and \(\arctan 1=\pi/4\). The substitution \(x=\tan\theta\) leaves

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\arctan^2 x}{x}\,dx=-\int_0^{\pi/4}2\theta\ln(\tan\theta)\,d\theta.
\]

On \((0,\pi/4)\), \(-\ln(\tan\theta)=2\sum_{k\ge 1}\cos\bigl(2(2k-1)\theta\bigr)/(2k-1)\). Integrating term by term,

\[
\int_0^{\pi/4}\theta\cos((4k-2)\theta)\,d\theta
=\frac{\pi}{8}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{2k-1}-\frac{1}{4(2k-1)^2},
\]

and multiplying by \(4/(2k-1)\) produces

\[
\sum_{k=1}^\infty\Biggl(\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(2k-1)^2}-\frac{1}{(2k-1)^3}\Biggr)
=\frac{\pi}{2}G-\frac78\zeta(3).
\]

Here \(G=\sum_{k\ge 0}(-1)^k/(2k+1)^2\) is Catalan's constant, and the odd zeta sum is \(\zeta(3)/8\) short of \(\zeta(3)\). The worked PDF's last line prints \(\pi G/2+7\zeta(3)/8\). The boundary terms above, and the mark scheme, have the minus sign.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi G}{2}-\frac78\zeta(3).
\]
