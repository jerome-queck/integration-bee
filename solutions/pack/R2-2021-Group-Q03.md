## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dx}{(2+\cos x)^{3}}.
\]

## Solution

For \(a>1\), the Weierstrass substitution \(t=\tan(x/2)\) gives
\[
J(a)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dx}{a+\cos x}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2\,dt}{(a+1)+(a-1)t^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}.
\]
Differentiating twice in the parameter,
\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dx}{(a+\cos x)^{3}}=\frac{1}{2}J''(a).
\]
At \(a=2\),
\[
\frac{1}{2}J''(2)=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{3}}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\pi/(2\sqrt{3})\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{3}}.
\]
