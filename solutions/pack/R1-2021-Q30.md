## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\arctan(x^2)}{1+x^2}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Integration by parts, as in the worked PDF, gives

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\arctan(x^2)}{1+x^2}\,dx=\frac{\pi^2}{16}-\int_0^1\frac{2x\arctan x}{1+x^4}\,dx.
\]

Write \(\arctan x=\int_0^1 x/(1+t^2 x^2)\,dt\) and clear the resulting rational factor with the identity

\[
x^2(1+t^4)+t^2(1+x^4)=(x^2+t^2)(1+x^2 t^2).
\]

The worked PDF reduces the remainder to

\[
I=2\int_0^1\frac{x^2}{1+x^4}\,dx\int_0^1\frac{1}{1+x^4}\,dx
\]

and quotes the partial-fraction values

\[
\int_0^1\frac{x^2}{1+x^4}\,dx=\frac{\pi+\ln(3-2\sqrt{2})}{4\sqrt{2}},\qquad
\int_0^1\frac{1}{1+x^4}\,dx=\frac{\pi-\ln(3-2\sqrt{2})}{4\sqrt{2}}.
\]

Their product is \(\pi^2/16-\frac1{16}\ln^2(3-2\sqrt{2})\), so the original integral is \(\frac1{16}\ln^2(3-2\sqrt{2})\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac1{16}\ln^2\bigl(3-2\sqrt{2}\bigr).
\]
