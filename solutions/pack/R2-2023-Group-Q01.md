## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\Bigl(\cos^{2}(\cos^{2}x)+\sin^{2}(\sin^{2}x)\Bigr)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Write \(f(x)=\cos^{2}(\cos^{2}x)+\sin^{2}(\sin^{2}x)\). Replacing \(x\) by \(\pi/2-x\) swaps \(\sin\) and \(\cos\), and
\[
f(x)+f\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}-x\Bigr)=2.
\]
Also \(f(x+\pi)=f(x)\), because sine and cosine change sign and both appearances are squared. Splitting \([0,\pi]\) into \([0,\pi/2]\) and \([\pi/2,\pi]\) therefore gives
\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}f(x)\,dx=\pi.
\]
The integrand is not identically \(1\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi\).

## Final answer

\[
\pi.
\]
