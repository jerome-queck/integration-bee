## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh x-x}{x^{2}\sinh x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Define \(h(x)=\coth x-1/x\) for \(x>0\). The half-angle identity
\(\coth(x/2)-\coth x=\operatorname{csch}x\) gives
\[
\frac{\sinh x-x}{x^{2}\sinh x}
=\frac{h(x)-h(x/2)}{x}.
\]
For \(0<\varepsilon<R/2\), substitute \(u=x/2\) in the second term
and cancel the overlapping finite intervals:
\[
\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{h(x)-h(x/2)}{x}\,dx
=\int_{R/2}^{R}\frac{h(u)}{u}\,du
-\int_{\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon}\frac{h(u)}{u}\,du.
\]
The endpoint limits are \(h(0^+)=0\) and \(h(+\infty)=1\).
After scaling each remaining interval to \([1/2,1]\), these endpoint
limits are uniform on that compact interval. The first term tends to
\(\int_{1/2}^{1}dv/v=\ln2\), and the second tends to \(0\).
Thus the ordinary improper integral is \(\ln2\), matching the mark scheme.

## Final answer

\[
\ln 2.
\]
