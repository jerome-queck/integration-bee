## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty\ln\frac{e^x+1}{e^x-1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

For \(x>0\) the worked PDF rewrites the integrand as \(\ln(1+e^{-x})-\ln(1-e^{-x})\) and expands both logarithms:

\[
\ln(1+e^{-x})=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}e^{-nx},\qquad
-\ln(1-e^{-x})=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n}e^{-nx}.
\]

Integrating term by term, \(\int_0^\infty e^{-nx}\,dx=1/n\), so the value is

\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^2}+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{12}+\frac{\pi^2}{6}=\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\]

The integrated series of \(1/n^2\) converges absolutely, which justifies the termwise integration on \((0,\infty)\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\]
