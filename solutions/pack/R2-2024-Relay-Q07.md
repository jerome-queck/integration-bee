## Problem

The product integral, from \(1\) to \(2\), of the \(x^{2}\)-th root of \(x-1\):
\[
\prod_{1}^{2}(x-1)^{1/x^{2}}.
\]
The sheet defines it by replacing the sum in a Riemann integral with a product.

## Solution

The logarithm of a product integral is the ordinary integral of the logarithm:
\[
\ln\prod_{1}^{2}(x-1)^{1/x^{2}}=\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\ln(x-1)}{x^{2}}\,dx.
\]
An antiderivative is
\[
\ln(x-1)-\frac{\ln(x-1)}{x}-\ln x.
\]
As \(x\downarrow 1\), \(t\ln t\to 0\) with \(t=x-1\), so the boundary term at \(1\) vanishes. At \(x=2\) the antiderivative equals \(-\ln 2\). Exponentiating gives \(1/2\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{1}{2}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy differentiates \(\ln(x-1)-\ln(x-1)/x-\ln x\) back to \(\ln(x-1)/x^{2}\), checks that the limit at \(1^{+}\) is \(0\), and evaluates \(\exp(-\ln 2)=1/2\).
