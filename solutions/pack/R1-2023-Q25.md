## Problem

\[
\int_0^{e^{1+e}}\frac{W(W(x))}{x}\,dx,
\]

where \(W\) is the inverse of \(u\mapsto u e^u\).

## Solution

Set \(u=W(x)\), so \(x=ue^u\) and \(dx=e^u(1+u)\,du\). The upper limit \(e^{1+e}\) has \(W(e^{1+e})=e\), and

\[
\int_0^e\frac{W(u)\,(1+u)}{u}\,du.
\]

Set \(v=W(u)\), so \(u=ve^v\) and the upper limit is \(W(e)=1\). The integrand becomes

\[
(1+v e^v)(1+v)=1+v+ve^v+v^2 e^v.
\]

Integrating from \(0\) to \(1\),

\[
\Bigl[v+\frac12 v^2+(v-1)e^v+(v^2-2v+2)e^v\Bigr]_0^1=e+\frac12.
\]

## Final answer

\[
e+\frac12.
\]
