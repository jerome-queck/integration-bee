## Problem

\[
\int\sqrt{1+\sin\frac{x}{2}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The half-angle identity in the worked PDF is

\[
1+\sin\frac{x}{2}=\Bigl(\sin\frac{x}{4}+\cos\frac{x}{4}\Bigr)^2,
\]

so

\[
\sqrt{1+\sin\frac{x}{2}}=\left|\sin\frac{x}{4}+\cos\frac{x}{4}\right|.
\]

On every interval where \(\sin(x/4)+\cos(x/4)\ge 0\), the integrand is \(\sin(x/4)+\cos(x/4)\). Its antiderivative is the expression printed on the answers sheet. That sign holds on an open interval about the origin, since \(\sin(x/4)+\cos(x/4)=\sqrt{2}\sin(x/4+\pi/4)\).

## Final answer

\[
4\Bigl(\sin\frac{x}{4}-\cos\frac{x}{4}\Bigr)+C
\]

on each interval where \(\sin(x/4)+\cos(x/4)\ge 0\).
