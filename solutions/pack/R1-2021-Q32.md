## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos(ax)-\cos(bx)}{x^2}\,dx,\qquad a>0,\; b>0.
\]

## Solution

Differentiate the cosine in a parameter:

\[
\cos(ax)-\cos(bx)=\int_a^b x\sin(tx)\,dt.
\]

Dividing by \(x^2\) and integrating in \(x\) produces

\[
\int_a^b\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin(tx)}{x}\,dx\,dt.
\]

For \(t>0\), the inner Dirichlet integral equals \(\pi/2\). Integrating the constant from \(a\) to \(b\) gives the mark-scheme value. The same formula remains valid when \(b<a\), since both sides change sign together.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi}{2}(b-a).
\]
