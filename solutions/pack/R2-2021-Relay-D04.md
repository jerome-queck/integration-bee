## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{x}{d-\cos^{2}x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Shuttle D3 passes \(d=\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2\). The double-angle identity gives
\[
\varphi-\cos^{2}x=\frac{\sqrt{5}-\cos 2x}{2},
\]
so the weight is \(g(x)=2/(\sqrt{5}-\cos 2x)\). The Weierstrass substitution on each half-period yields, for \(a>1\),
\[
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a-\cos\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}.
\]
Two full turns of \(\cos 2x\) therefore give \(\int_{0}^{2\pi}g=2\pi\). The weight is symmetric about \(x=\pi\), so
\[
\int_{0}^{2\pi}xg(x)\,dx=\pi\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x)\,dx=2\pi^{2}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(2\pi^{2}\).

## Final answer

\[
2\pi^{2}.
\]
