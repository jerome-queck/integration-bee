## Problem

\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1-\sin^2 x}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The square root is nonnegative, so \(\sqrt{1-\sin^2 x}=|\cos x|\). The integrand \(1/(1+|\cos x|)\) is not periodic of period \(\pi/2\), because \(|\cos(x+\pi/2)|=|\sin x|\). Each quadrant integrates to \(1\): on \([0,\pi/2]\), \(1/(1+\cos x)=\frac12\sec^2(x/2)\), and on \([\pi/2,\pi]\), \(1/(1-\cos x)=\frac12\csc^2(x/2)\). The four quadrants sum to \(4\). The identity \(1+\cos x=2\cos^2(x/2)\) is the first quadrant only:

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{1+\cos x}=\Bigl[\tan\frac x2\Bigr]_0^{\pi/2}=1.
\]

The upper limit is \(\tan(\pi/4)=1\). The worked PDF notes that the absolute value is required for the original square root.

## Final answer

\[
4.
\]
