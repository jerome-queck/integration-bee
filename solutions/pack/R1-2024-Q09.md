## Problem

\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1-\sin^2 x}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The square root is nonnegative, so \(\sqrt{1-\sin^2 x}=|\cos x|\). The integrand has period \(\pi/2\) after accounting for that absolute value:

\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{dx}{1+|\cos x|}=4\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{1+\cos x}.
\]

Then \(1+\cos x=2\cos^2(x/2)\), and

\[
4\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{1}{2}\sec^2\frac x2\,dx=4\Bigl[\tan\frac x2\Bigr]_0^{\pi/2}=4.
\]

The upper limit is \(\tan(\pi/4)=1\). The worked PDF notes that the absolute value is required for the original square root.

## Final answer

\[
4.
\]
