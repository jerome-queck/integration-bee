## Problem

\[
\int\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{4}+1}\,dt.
\]

## Solution

On \((0,\infty)\) and on \((-\infty,0)\) separately, set
\[
u=\frac{t-1/t}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]
Then
\[
du=\frac{t^{2}+1}{\sqrt{2}\,t^{2}}\,dt,\qquad t^{4}+1=t^{2}\bigl((t-1/t)^{2}+2\bigr)=2t^{2}(u^{2}+1),
\]
and the integrand collapses to
\[
\frac{du}{\sqrt{2}\,(u^{2}+1)}.
\]
The antiderivative is
\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\arctan\Biggl(\frac{t-t^{-1}}{\sqrt{2}}\Biggr)+C
\]
on each of those two intervals. The mark scheme prints that expression.

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\arctan\Biggl(\dfrac{t-t^{-1}}{\sqrt{2}}\Biggr)+C.
\]

## SymPy self-check

For \(t>0\), SymPy simplifies the derivative of \(\arctan((t-1/t)/\sqrt{2})/\sqrt{2}\) minus \((t^{2}+1)/(t^{4}+1)\) to \(0\).
