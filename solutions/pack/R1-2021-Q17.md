## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^3 x}{2+\sin x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Divide \(s^3\) by \(s+2\): \(s^3/(s+2)=s^2-2s+4-8/(s+2)\). The elementary pieces on \([0,\pi/2]\) are \(\int\sin^2 x=\pi/4\), \(\int\sin x=1\), and \(\int 4=2\pi\). The Weierstrass substitution \(t=\tan(x/2)\) turns the remaining piece into
\[
\int_0^1\frac{dt}{t^2+t+1}=\frac\pi{3\sqrt{3}}.
\]
Combining these contributions gives
\[
\frac{9\pi}{4}-2-\frac{8\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}=\frac{\pi(81-32\sqrt{3})}{36}-2.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\pi(81-32\sqrt{3})/36-2\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi(81-32\sqrt{3})}{36}-2.
\]
