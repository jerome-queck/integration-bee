## Problem

\[
\int_{1/8}^{1/4}\frac{\ln(2x)\ln(4x)\ln(8x)}{x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Set \(x=2^{-u}\). Then \(dx/x=-\ln 2\,du\), and \(u\) runs from \(3\) down to \(2\). The logarithms are
\[
\ln(2x)=(1-u)\ln 2,\qquad \ln(4x)=(2-u)\ln 2,\qquad \ln(8x)=(3-u)\ln 2.
\]
The integral becomes
\[
(\ln 2)^{4}\int_{2}^{3}(u-1)(u-2)(u-3)\,(-1)\,du.
\]
The further shift \(v=u-2\) runs from \(0\) to \(1\) and the integrand is \(v(1-v^{2})\):
\[
(\ln 2)^{4}\int_{0}^{1}v(1-v^{2})\,dv=\frac{(\ln 2)^{4}}{4}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \((\ln 2)^{4}/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{(\ln 2)^{4}}{4}.
\]
