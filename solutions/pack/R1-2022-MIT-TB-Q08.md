## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{1}{\tan^{\sqrt{2}} x+1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Let \(p\) be any real number and set \(f(x)=1/(1+\tan^p x)\). The reflection \(x\mapsto\pi/2-x\) replaces \(\tan x\) by \(\cot x\), and
\[
f(x)+\frac{1}{1+\cot^p x}=1
\]
on \((0,\pi/2)\). The two resulting integrals are equal, so each is half of \(\int_0^{\pi/2}1\,dx\):
\[
\frac12\cdot\frac\pi2=\frac\pi4.
\]
The same value holds for the printed exponent \(\sqrt{2}\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi/4\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac\pi4.
\]
