## Integral and intended pass-on

The integrand is \((1-x)/(2x+1)\). Its integral is
\[
\frac{3}{4}\ln 3-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{4}(\ln 27-2).
\]
An antiderivative is \(-x/2+(3/4)\ln(2x+1)\); its derivative is the
integrand and its value at \(0\) is zero. The normalized representation is
\(r=1\), \(s=4\), \(t=27\), and \(u=2\), giving
\[
\frac{r+\sqrt{r+s}}{u}=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2\).

## Why the printed conditions need normalization

The stated conditions do not uniquely determine the pass-on value. The original
problem (shuttle PDF, page 5) requires positive integers \(r,s,t,u\), with
\(r\) and \(s\) coprime, but it does not prescribe a normalization of the
logarithm. The same integral also equals
\[
\frac18(\ln729-4).
\]
This gives \((r,s,t,u)=(1,8,729,4)\), which satisfies every printed condition,
but its pass-on value is \((1+\sqrt{1+8})/4=1\). Thus the mark scheme's
golden ratio (page 4, shuttle D3) is obtained from the intended representation
above, while another allowed representation gives a different value. The
alternate tuple has \(u=4\), so the catalog's explicit minimal-\(u\) rule
rejects it in favor of the tuple with \(u=2\). Its pass-on \(1\) is not
accepted for the clarified graded problem.

## Why the chosen normalization is unique

There is a short exact classification of all the printed representations.
Suppose
\[
\frac rs(\ln t-u)=\frac34\ln3-\frac12.
\]
Clearing denominators and exponentiating gives
\[
\frac{t^{4r}}{3^{3s}}=e^{4ru-2s}.
\]
The left side is rational. By the classical transcendence of \(e\), an
integer power \(e^m\) can be rational only when \(m=0\): otherwise
\(e\) would satisfy a polynomial equation with rational coefficients.
Hence \(4ru-2s=0\), so \(s=2ru\). Coprimality of \(r\) and
\(s\) forces \(r=1\), and then \(s=2u\).

The remaining equality is \(t^2=3^{3u}\). Unique prime factorization
forces \(u\) to be even. Writing \(u=2k\), every allowed tuple is
therefore
\[
(r,s,t,u)=(1,4k,27^k,2k),\qquad k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
Conversely, every displayed tuple has the required positivity and coprimality
and gives the same integral, by \(\ln(27^k)=k\ln27\). The minimum is
\(u=2\), attained only at \(k=1\). This proves the clarified pass-on
is unique. The transcendence theorem is an explicit mathematical premise of
this argument; SymPy is used to check the algebra and evaluations, not to
prove that theorem.

The selected \(t=27\) is a cube and is not square-free; a square-free
condition would not describe the marked tuple.

## Certified final answer

\[
\dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}.
\]
This is the mark scheme's intended value and the unique pass-on under the
stated catalog normalization. It is not claimed to be uniquely determined
by the unamended printed conditions.
