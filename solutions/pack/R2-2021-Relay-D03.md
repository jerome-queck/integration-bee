## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x}{x^{c}+(x^{c}+1)^{c}}\,dx,
\]

## Solution

in the form \((r/s)(\ln t-u)\), and pass on \((r+\sqrt{r+s})/u\). Shuttle D2 passes \(c=1\).

The integrand is \((1-x)/(2x+1)\). Its integral is
\[
\frac{3}{4}\ln 3-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{4}(\ln 27-2).
\]
Thus \(r=1\), \(s=4\), \(t=27\), and \(u=2\), and
\[
\frac{r+\sqrt{r+s}}{u}=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2\).

## Why the catalog remains manual

The stated conditions do not uniquely determine the pass-on value. The original
problem (shuttle PDF, page 5) requires positive integers \(r,s,t,u\), with
\(r\) and \(s\) coprime, but it does not prescribe a normalization of the
logarithm. The same integral also equals
\[
\frac18(\ln729-4).
\]
This gives \((r,s,t,u)=(1,8,729,4)\), which satisfies every printed condition,
but its pass-on value is \((1+\sqrt{1+8})/4=1\). Thus the mark scheme's
golden ratio (page 4, shuttle D3) is obtained from the intended representation
above, while another allowed representation gives a different value. A scalar
certificate for the unique printed answer would therefore overstate the source.

## Printed final answer

\[
\dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}.
\]
This is the mark scheme's intended value, not a uniquely determined consequence
of the printed conditions.
