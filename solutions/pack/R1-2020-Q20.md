## Problem

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}(\ln\tan\theta)^2\,d\theta.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(u=\tan\theta\) from the worked PDF gives

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}(\ln\tan\theta)^2\,d\theta=\int_0^\infty\frac{\ln^2 u}{1+u^2}\,du.
\]

The further substitution \(u=1/t\) shows that the integrals over \((0,1)\) and \((1,\infty)\) are equal, so the value is

\[
2\int_0^1\frac{\ln^2 u}{1+u^2}\,du.
\]

Integrate by parts with \(v=\ln^2 u\) and \(dw=du/(1+u^2)\). The boundary term \(\ln^2 u\cdot\arctan u\) vanishes at both ends, and

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\ln^2 u}{1+u^2}\,du=-2\int_0^1\frac{\ln u\cdot\arctan u}{u}\,du.
\]

Insert \(\arctan u=\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-1)^n u^{2n+1}/(2n+1)\) and use \(\int_0^1(-\ln u)\,u^{2n}\,du=1/(2n+1)^2\). The original integral becomes

\[
4\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^3}.
\]

That series equals \(\pi^3/8\), which is the value printed on the answers sheet. The quadrature below checks the tangent-substituted integral directly.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^3}{8}.
\]
