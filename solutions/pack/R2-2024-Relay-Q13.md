## Problem

\[
\int_{1/4}^{1/2}(1+\ln x)\ln(1+x^{x})\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Set \(y=x^{x}\). Logarithmic differentiation gives \((1+\ln x)\,dx=dy/y\), and \(y>0\) throughout the interval. The integral becomes
\[
\int\frac{\ln(1+y)}{y}\,dy
\]
along the path traced by \(x^{x}\). The endpoints agree:
\[
\Bigl(\frac12\Bigr)^{1/2}=\Bigl(\frac14\Bigr)^{1/4}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]
An antiderivative in \(y\) therefore takes the same value at both ends, and the integral is \(0\).

0.
