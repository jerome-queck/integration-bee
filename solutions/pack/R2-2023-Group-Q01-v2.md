## Problem

\[
I=\int_0^\pi\left(\cos^2(\cos^2 x)+\sin^2(\sin^2 x)\right)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Use the parent derivation with
\(f(x)=\cos^2(\cos^2 x)+\sin^2(\sin^2 x)\). The complementary-angle
identity and the period are
\[
f(x)+f(\pi/2-x)=2,\qquad f(x+\pi)=f(x).
\]
The substitution \(u=\pi/2-x\) sends the integral of \(f(\pi/2-x)\)
over \([0,\pi]\) to the integral of \(f(u)\) over
\([-\pi/2,\pi/2]\). Periodicity makes this equal to \(I\): translate
the negative half by \(\pi\) and join the two halves into \([0,\pi]\).
Integrating the complementary-angle identity therefore gives
\(2I=2\pi\). This agrees with the printed Group Round answer.
