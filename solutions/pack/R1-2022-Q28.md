## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\ln\Biggl(\frac{\sqrt{1+x}+1}{\sqrt{1+x}-1}\Biggr)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(x=\cos(2t)\) gives

\[
I=2\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\Biggl(\frac{\sqrt2\cos t+1}{\sqrt2\cos t-1}\Biggr)\,dt.
\]

Set \(A=\pi/8+t/2\) and \(B=\pi/8-t/2\). Product-to-sum gives

\[
\sqrt2\cos t+1=2\sqrt2\cos A\cos B,
\qquad
\sqrt2\cos t-1=2\sqrt2\sin A\sin B.
\]

All factors are positive in the open interval, so the logarithm equals \(-\ln\tan A-\ln\tan B\). The two affine substitutions cover adjacent halves of \([0,\pi/4]\), giving

\[
I=-4\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(\tan u)\,du=4G.
\]

The last equality follows from \(v=\tan u\) and the geometric series of \(1/(1+v^2)\): \(G=\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^n/(2n+1)^2\). The mark scheme prints \(4G\).

## Final answer

\[
4G.
\]
