## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\ln\Biggl(\frac{\sqrt{1+x}+1}{\sqrt{1+x}-1}\Biggr)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(x=\cos\theta\) produces

\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\Biggl(\frac{\sqrt{1+\cos\theta}+1}{\sqrt{1+\cos\theta}-1}\Biggr)\,d\theta.
\]

Rationalising the fraction and using \(1+\cos\theta=2\cos^2(\theta/2)\) gives

\[
4\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln\bigl(\cos(\pi/4)+\cos\phi\bigr)\,d\phi+2\pi\ln 2,
\]

up to the constant rearrangement printed in the worked PDF. The sum-to-product identity and the Fourier series

\[
\ln(\cos\psi)=-\ln 2+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\cos(2n\psi)
\]

reduce the integral to

\[
4\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^2}=4G,
\]

where \(G\) is Catalan's constant. The mark scheme prints \(4G\).

## Final answer

\[
4G.
\]
