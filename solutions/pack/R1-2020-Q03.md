## Problem

\[
\int_0^{1/2}\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt[4]{(1+x)^2(1-x)^6}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Substitute \(x=\cos 2\theta\). Then \(1+x=2\cos^2\theta\) and \(1-x=2\sin^2\theta\), so the fourth root is \(4\sin^3\theta\cos\theta\) for \(\theta\in(\pi/6,\pi/4)\). Also \(dx=-4\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta\), and \(x:0\to 1/2\) runs \(\theta:\pi/4\to\pi/6\). The integrand collapses to \(-(1+\sqrt{3})\csc^2\theta\), and reversing the limits gives
\[
(1+\sqrt{3})\int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/4}\csc^2\theta\,d\theta=(1+\sqrt{3})\bigl(\cot(\pi/6)-\cot(\pi/4)\bigr)=(1+\sqrt{3})(\sqrt{3}-1)=2.
\]
The answers sheet prints \(2\).

## Final answer

\[
2.
\]
