## Problem

\[
\int\frac{x-1}{(x+1)\sqrt{x^3+x^2+x}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF sets \(u=\sqrt{x}\). Then

\[
\int\frac{x-1}{(x+1)\sqrt{x^3+x^2+x}}\,dx
=2\int\frac{u^2-1}{(u^2+1)\sqrt{u^4+u^2+1}}\,du
=2\int\frac{1-u^{-2}}{(u+u^{-1})\sqrt{(u+u^{-1})^2-1}}\,du.
\]

The further substitution \(u+u^{-1}=\sec\theta\) collapses the integrand to \(2\), so the antiderivative is \(2\theta\), that is

\[
2\operatorname{arcsec}\Bigl(\sqrt{x}+\frac1{\sqrt{x}}\Bigr)+C.
\]

The mark scheme prints the equivalent form

\[
2\arctan\sqrt{\frac{x^2+x+1}{x}}+C.
\]

On \(x>0\) the two expressions differ by the constant \(0\).

## Final answer

\[
2\arctan\sqrt{\frac{x^2+x+1}{x}}+C,
\]

which is the same antiderivative as \(2\operatorname{arcsec}(\sqrt{x}+x^{-1/2})+C\).
