## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty\left(\frac{\ln x}{1+x}\right)^2\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Split at \(1\). The substitution \(u=1/x\) on \((0,1)\) doubles the integral over \((1,\infty)\). Integration by parts, then the same substitution, produces
\[
4\int_0^1\frac{-\ln x}{1+x}\,dx.
\]
Expand \(1/(1+x)=\sum_{n\ge 0}(-1)^n x^n\) and set \(u=-\ln x\). Each term is a Gamma value:
\[
4\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{(n+1)^2}=4\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{12}=\frac{\pi^2}{3}.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\pi^2/3\).

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^2}{3}.
\]
