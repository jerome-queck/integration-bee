## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\ln(\sin x)\cos^{3}x\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Shift the variable by \(t=x-\pi/2\). Then \(\sin x=\cos t\) and \(\cos x=-\sin t\), and the interval becomes \([-\pi/2,\pi/2]\). The integral is
\[
-\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\ln(\cos t)\sin^{3}t\,dt.
\]
The integrand is odd, and the interval is symmetric, so the integral is \(0\).

## Final answer

\[
0.
\]
