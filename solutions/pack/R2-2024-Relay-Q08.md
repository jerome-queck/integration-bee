## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\Biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-\sec^{2}x}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-\tan^{2}x}}\Biggr)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The identity \(\sec^{2}x=\tan^{2}x+1\) makes the general term a difference
\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt{(n-1)-\tan^{2}x}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-\tan^{2}x}}.
\]
The series telescopes. On \([0,\pi/4)\) one has \(\tan x<1\), so the remainder at infinity vanishes and the sum equals \(1/\sqrt{1-\tan^{2}x}\). Put \(u=\tan x\). Then \(du=\sec^{2}x\,dx=(1+u^{2})\,dx\) and
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{(1+u^{2})\sqrt{1-u^{2}}}.
\]
The further substitution \(u=\sin\theta\) cancels the cosine factor and leaves
\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{1+\sin^{2}\theta}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\sec^{2}\theta}{1+2\tan^{2}\theta}\,d\theta.
\]
With \(t=\tan\theta\) the limits run from \(0\) to \(\infty\), and
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{1+2t^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}.
\]

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}.
\]

## SymPy self-check

SymPy simplifies \(\sec^{2}\theta/(1+2\tan^{2}\theta)-1/(1+\sin^{2}\theta)\) to \(0\), and evaluates \(\int_{0}^{\infty}dt/(1+2t^{2})=\pi/(2\sqrt{2})\).
