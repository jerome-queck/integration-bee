## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln(1-x+x^{2})}{(1+x^{2})\ln x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The integrand has a removable singularity at \(x=1\). Substitute \(x=1/t\) on \((0,\infty)\), reversing the limits. The pulled-back integrand is
\[
-\frac{\ln(1-t+t^{2})}{(1+t^{2})\ln t}+\frac{2}{1+t^{2}}.
\]
The original integral \(I\) therefore satisfies \(I=-I+\int_{0}^{\infty}2/(1+t^{2})\,dt=-I+\pi\), so \(I=\pi/2\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi/2\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{2}.
\]
