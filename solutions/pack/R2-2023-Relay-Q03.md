## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{2}\ln\Gamma(2-x)\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(t=2-x\) rewrites the integral as \(\int_{0}^{2}\ln\Gamma(t)\,dt\). The reflection formula \(\Gamma(t)\Gamma(1-t)=\pi/\sin(\pi t)\) gives
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\ln\Gamma(t)\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}\ln\Gamma(1-t)\,dt=\ln\pi-\int_{0}^{1}\ln\sin(\pi t)\,dt.
\]
The two gamma integrals are equal, and \(\int_{0}^{1}\ln\sin(\pi t)\,dt=-\ln 2\), so
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\ln\Gamma(t)\,dt=\frac12\ln(2\pi).
\]
On \([1,2]\), \(\Gamma(t)=(t-1)\Gamma(t-1)\), and
\[
\int_{1}^{2}\ln\Gamma(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\ln u+\ln\Gamma(u)\bigr)\,du=-1+\frac12\ln(2\pi).
\]
Adding the two pieces yields \(\ln(2\pi)-1\). The mark scheme prints \(\log(2\pi)-1\).

## Final answer

\[
\log(2\pi)-1.
\]
