## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\arctan\left(\frac{1+\tan x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)dx.
\]

## Solution

The solutions PDF rewrites the argument of the arctangent:
\[
\frac{1+\tan x}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\sin x+\cos x}{\sqrt{2}\cos x}=\frac{\cos(x-\pi/4)}{\cos x}.
\]
The substitution \(u=\pi/4-x\) replaces this argument by its reciprocal. The two arctangents therefore sum to \(\pi/2\), and
\[
2I=\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\frac{\pi}{2}\,dx=\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}.
\]
Hence \(I=\pi^{2}/16\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi^{2}/16\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{16}.
\]
