## Problem

\[
\int\frac{1}{1-\sin x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Differentiate the sum of secant and tangent:
\[
\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x+\tan x)=\sec x(\sec x+\tan x)=\frac{1+\sin x}{\cos^2 x}.
\]
Multiplying by the conjugate factor \(1-\sin x\) gives
\[
\frac{1+\sin x}{\cos^2 x}\cdot(1-\sin x)=\frac{1-\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x}=1.
\]
Hence \(\sec x+\tan x\) is an antiderivative of \(1/(1-\sin x)\). The mark scheme prints \(\sec x+\tan x\).

## Final answer

\[
\sec x+\tan x.
\]
