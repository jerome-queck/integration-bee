## Problem

\[
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\cos x}{1+e^{-1/x}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

For \(x>0\),
\[
\frac{1}{1+e^{-1/x}}+\frac{1}{1+e^{1/x}}=1.
\]
Cosine is even, so the integrand \(f\) satisfies \(f(x)+f(-x)=\cos x\) for \(x\in(0,1]\). The two-sided singularity at \(0\) is removable in this sum. Therefore
\[
\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}\cos x\,dx=\sin 1.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\sin 1\).

## Final answer

\[
\sin 1.
\]
