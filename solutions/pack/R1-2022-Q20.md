## Problem

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\ln(1+x+x^2)}{x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF uses \(1+x+x^2=(1-x^3)/(1-x)\):

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\ln(1-x^3)}{x}\,dx-\int_0^1\frac{\ln(1-x)}{x}\,dx.
\]

The series \(\ln(1-y)=-\sum_{n\ge 1}y^n/n\), integrated term by term, gives

\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Biggl(-\frac{1}{3n^2}+\frac{1}{n^2}\Biggr)=\Bigl(1-\frac13\Bigr)\frac{\pi^2}{6}=\frac{\pi^2}{9}.
\]

The integrated series of \(x^{n-1}/n\) and \(x^{3n-1}/n\) converge absolutely on \([0,1-\varepsilon]\) and the limit \(\varepsilon\to 0\) is the Basel value above.

## Final answer

\[
\frac{\pi^2}{9}.
\]
