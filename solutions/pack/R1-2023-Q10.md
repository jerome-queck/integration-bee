## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sin(a\ln x)}{\ln x}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The worked PDF treats the integral as a function \(I(a)\) and substitutes \(u=-\ln x\):
\[
I(a)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}\sin(au)}{u}\,du.
\]
Differentiating under the integral sign gives
\[
I'(a)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}\cos(au)\,du=\frac{1}{1+a^{2}}
\]
for \(a>0\). Also \(I(0)=0\), so
\[
I(a)=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}=\arctan a.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(\arctan a\).

## Final answer

\[
\arctan a.
\]
