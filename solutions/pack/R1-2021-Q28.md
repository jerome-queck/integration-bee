## Problem

\[
\int_1^{\infty}\frac{1}{x+x^{\sqrt{2}}}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

Divide the numerator and denominator by \(x^{\sqrt{2}}\):
\[
\int_1^{\infty}\frac{x^{-\sqrt{2}}}{x^{1-\sqrt{2}}+1}\,dx.
\]
The substitution \(u=x^{1-\sqrt{2}}\) has \(du=(1-\sqrt{2})x^{-\sqrt{2}}\,dx\), so the antiderivative is
\[
\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{2}}\ln\bigl(x^{1-\sqrt{2}}+1\bigr).
\]
As \(x\to\infty\), the power \(1-\sqrt{2}\) is negative and the logarithm tends to \(\ln 1=0\). At \(x=1\) the value is \(\ln 2/(1-\sqrt{2})\), so the definite integral is
\[
-\frac{\ln 2}{1-\sqrt{2}}=(1+\sqrt{2})\ln 2.
\]
The mark scheme prints \((\sqrt{2}+1)\ln 2\).

## Final answer

\[
(1+\sqrt{2})\ln 2.
\]
