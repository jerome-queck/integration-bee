## Problem

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\ln(1+x)}{x^2+1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(x=\tan\theta\) converts the integral into \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(1+\tan\theta)\,d\theta\). Using \(1+\tan\theta=\sqrt{2}\sin(\theta+\pi/4)/\cos\theta\) and the evaluation \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\cos\theta)\,d\theta=-(\pi/2)\ln 2\) shifts the interval to \([-\pi/4,\pi/4]\) and leaves
\[
2A+\frac{3\pi}{4}\ln 2,\qquad A=\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(\cos\theta)\,d\theta.
\]
Let \(B=\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(\sin\theta)\,d\theta\). Then \(A+B=-(\pi/2)\ln 2\) and \(B-A=\int_0^1\ln x/(1+x^2)\,dx=-G\), where \(G\) is Catalan's constant. Solving for \(2A\) produces
\[
G+\frac\pi4\ln 2.
\]
The mark scheme prints \(G+\pi\ln 2/4\).

## Final answer

\[
G+\frac{\pi\ln 2}{4}.
\]
