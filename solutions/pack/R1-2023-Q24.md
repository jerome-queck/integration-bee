## Problem

\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{4}-x^{2}+1}\,dx.
\]

## Solution

The substitution \(u=1/x\) in the worked PDF returns the same integral with numerator \(x^{2}\). Adding the two copies,
\[
2I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2}+1}{x^{4}-x^{2}+1}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1+x^{-2}}{\bigl(x-1/x\bigr)^{2}+1}\,dx.
\]
The antiderivative is \(\arctan(x-1/x)\). It runs from \(-\pi/2\) to \(\pi/2\), so \(2I=\pi\) and \(I=\pi/2\). The mark scheme prints \(\pi/2\).

## Final answer

\[
\dfrac{\pi}{2}.
\]
