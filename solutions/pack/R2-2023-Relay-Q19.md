\(\sqrt n\) bound is an intentional problem clarification inferred from

The complete limit of the clarified finite integral is stored in

## Certificate for the clarified contest reading

For \(0\leq u\leq1<\pi/2\), the cosine is positive. Also
\[
\frac{d}{du}(\tan u-u)=\tan^2u\geq0,
\qquad (\tan u-u)\big|_{u=0}=0.
\]
Consequently \(\tan u\geq u\), and
\[
\frac{d}{du}\left(\log\cos u+\frac{u^2}{2}\right)
=-\tan u+u\leq0.
\]
The expression in parentheses vanishes at zero. Therefore
\(\log\cos u\leq-u^2/2\) on this whole interval.

Extend the integrand defining \(J_n\) by zero beyond \(\sqrt n\):
\[
g_n(x)=
\begin{cases}
\cos^n(x/\sqrt n),&0\leq x\leq\sqrt n,\\
0,&x>\sqrt n.
\end{cases}
\]
The upper-bound clarification is essential here: it ensures
\(0\leq x/\sqrt n\leq1\), so
\[
0\leq g_n(x)\leq e^{-x^2/2}\qquad(x\geq0).
\]
Every fixed \(x\geq0\) eventually lies inside the integration interval,
and the exact exponent limit is
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\log\cos(x/\sqrt n)=-\frac{x^2}{2}.
\]
Thus \(g_n(x)\to e^{-x^2/2}\). The dominating function is integrable,
so dominated convergence gives
\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}J_n
=\int_0^\infty e^{-x^2/2}\,dx
=\sqrt{\frac\pi2}.}
\]
The certificate checks the full finite-integral shape, positivity of the
cosine on the scaled interval, both derivative identities, the pointwise
exponent limit, and the finite Gaussian mass. It never applies the limit
exchange to the printed \(n\)-bound interval.

## An even subsequence contradicts the printed finite value

Take a positive even integer \(n\). For every integer
\[
1\leq k\leq K_n
 :=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{\pi\sqrt n}\right\rfloor,
\]
the interval \([k\pi\sqrt n-1,k\pi\sqrt n+1]\) is contained in
\([0,n]\). These intervals are disjoint. Write
\(x=k\pi\sqrt n+v\) with \(|v|\leq1\). Since \(n\) is even,
\[
\cos^n\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt n}\right)
 =\cos^n\!\left(\frac v{\sqrt n}\right).
\]
The inequalities \(\cos t\geq1-t^2/2\) and
\((1-a)^n\geq1-na\) for \(0\leq a\leq1\) give
\[
\cos^n\!\left(\frac v{\sqrt n}\right)
 \geq\left(1-\frac1{2n}\right)^n\geq\frac12.
\]
Each interval therefore contributes at least \(1\). The integrand is
nonnegative everywhere for even \(n\), so
\[
I_n\geq K_n\geq\frac{n-1}{\pi\sqrt n}-1
 \longrightarrow+\infty
 \qquad(n\to\infty\text{ through even integers}).
\]
Thus the printed sequence cannot converge to \(\sqrt{\pi/2}\).

## Why the pointwise Gaussian argument is insufficient

For fixed \(x\),
\(\cos^n(x/\sqrt n)\to e^{-x^2/2}\). This describes the first
peak near the origin. The expanding integration interval also contains
peaks near \(k\pi\sqrt n\). Their accumulated contribution invalidates
an interchange of limit and integral over the whole printed interval.

In fact the odd subsequence is bounded. Set
\(A_n=\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^n t\,dt\) and
\(H_n(s)=\int_0^s\cos^n t\,dt\). For odd \(n\), reflection
about \(\pi/2\) gives \(H_n(\pi)=0\), and the shift by \(\pi\)
gives \(H_n(s+\pi)=-H_n(s)\). On \([0,\pi]\), \(H_n\)
first increases from \(0\) to \(A_n\), then decreases back to \(0\).
Consequently,
\[
\left|\int_0^s\cos^n t\,dt\right|\leq A_n
\quad\text{for every }s\geq0.
\]
On \([0,\pi/2)\), \(\log\cos t\leq-t^2/2\), since
\(\tan t\geq t\). Hence
\[
|I_n|\leq\sqrt n\,A_n
 \leq\sqrt n\int_0^\infty e^{-nt^2/2}\,dt
 =\sqrt{\frac\pi2}
\quad(n\text{ odd}).
\]
Together with the even subsequence, this proves that the limit as printed
does not exist, even as an extended real limit. This is a conflict between
the supplied problem and final, not a lack of a closed-form technique.
This argument concerns the original printed statement, separately from the
explicitly clarified graded statement.

## SymPy self-check

`verify/pack_handoff_m.py` recognizes the complete clarified limit expression
and derives its Gaussian mass without trusting the row id or the proposed
answer. The expression contract accepts \(\sqrt{\pi/2}\) and rejects
`0`, `1`, `sqrt(pi)`, and `pi/2`. Changing its upper bound back to the
printed \(n\), changing its kernel, or omitting the integer-index
assumption does not match the certificate.

The source-discrepancy diagnostics remain useful: exact checks verify the
cosine shift and parity identities, the Bernoulli induction identity
\((1-a)(1-na)-(1-(n+1)a)=na^2\), and the Gaussian bound on the
odd subsequence. They return exactly \(+\infty\) for
\(\lim_{m\to\infty}((2m-1)/(\pi\sqrt{2m})-1)\), and exactly
\(\sqrt{\pi/2}\) for
\(\sqrt n\int_0^\infty e^{-nt^2/2}\,dt\). These diagnose why the
literal source statement is excluded; they are not used to assert that its
limit equals the printed finite answer.
