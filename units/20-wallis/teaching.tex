\section{Learning goals}
Set up a family $I_n$ and reduce the index by a fixed step.
Guide section 1.3 is this move. The Wallis product in guide section 1.4 is what the reduction can be used for later.

\section{Worked reduction --- \texttt{U20-D01}}
Write
\[
I_n=\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^n x\,dx,\qquad n\ge 0.
\]
The base is $I_0=\pi/2$. For $n\ge 2$, integration by parts gives
\[
I_n=\frac{n-1}{n}I_{n-2}.
\]
The boundary term on $[0,\pi/2]$ vanishes.
Then $I_2=\pi/4$ and
\[
I_4=\frac34\cdot\frac\pi4=\frac{3\pi}{16},
\]
which is the stored answer of \texttt{U20-D01}.
The same step with sine in place of cosine gives the odd powers. $I_1=1$ for sine, and $I_5=8/15$, the stored answer of \texttt{U20-D04}.

A second family, not one of these drills, is $\int_0^\infty x^n e^{-x}\,dx=n\int_0^\infty x^{n-1}e^{-x}\,dx$ for a positive integer $n$, with the $n=0$ case equal to 1. Reduce until the index is 0 or 1, then stop.

\section{Faded step --- \texttt{U20-D02}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2 x\cos^2 x\,dx.
\]
Use $\sin x\cos x=\frac12\sin 2x$, square it, and reduce $\sin^2(2x)$.
The stored answer is $\pi/16$.
