\section{Learning goals}
Set up a family $I_n$ and reduce the index by a fixed step.
Stop at a base you can compute. Do not start a reduction when a single substitution finishes the integral.

\section{The pattern}
A contest recurrence has an integer $n$ in the integrand and the same shape at $n$ and at $n-1$ or $n-2$. Define
\[
I_n=\int_c^d f_n(x)\,dx
\]
with the limits independent of $n$. Produce a relation $I_n = a_n I_{n-k}+b_n$ by parts or by an identity, compute $I_0$ or $I_1$ directly, and unroll. The boundary term is the part that usually vanishes. Write it down before you drop it.

This is not a series (unit 08) and not a derivative in a continuous parameter (unit 34 and \texttt{docs/techniques/introduce-constant.md}). The index jumps by a fixed integer.

\section{Worked reduction --- \texttt{U20-D01}}
Write
\[
I_n=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^n x\,dx=\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^n x\,dx,\qquad n\ge 0.
\]
The two families agree by $x\mapsto\pi/2-x$. The base cases are $I_0=\pi/2$ and $I_1=1$.

For $n\ge 2$, integrate $\sin^n x=\sin^{n-1}x\cdot\sin x$ by parts on $[0,\pi/2]$. The boundary term $\sin^{n-1}x\cos x$ is 0 at both ends. What remains is
\[
I_n=\frac{n-1}{n}I_{n-2}.
\]
The same step with cosine in place of sine gives the same relation. Then $I_2=\pi/4$ and
\[
I_4=\frac34\cdot\frac\pi4=\frac{3\pi}{16},
\]
which is the stored answer of \texttt{U20-D01} and of \texttt{U20-D07}.
Keep going: $I_6=(5/6)I_4=5\pi/32$, the stored answer of \texttt{U20-D05}.
For the odd line, $I_3=(2/3)I_1=2/3$ and $I_5=(4/5)I_3=8/15$, the stored answer of \texttt{U20-D04}. \texttt{U20-D08} is $I_3$ for cosine, stored value $2/3$.

\section{Faded step --- \texttt{U20-D02}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2 x\cos^2 x\,dx.
\]
Use $\sin x\cos x=\frac12\sin 2x$, square it, and reduce $\sin^2(2x)=(1-\cos 4x)/2$. The integrand is $(1-\cos 4x)/8$. The stored answer is $\pi/16$. You do not need the general $I_n$ once a double angle has removed the product.

\section{Expand, then use the values you have}
\texttt{U20-D03} is $\int_0^{\pi/2}(1+\sin x)^3\,dx$. Expand
\[
(1+\sin x)^3=1+3\sin x+3\sin^2 x+\sin^3 x
\]
and insert $I_0$, $I_1$, $I_2$, $I_3$:
\[
\frac\pi2 + 3\cdot 1 + 3\cdot\frac\pi4 + \frac23 = \frac{11}{3}+\frac{5\pi}{4}.
\]
That is the stored answer. A binomial around a sine power is still this unit.

\texttt{U20-D06} saves one sine: $\sin^3 x\cos^2 x=\sin x(1-\cos^2 x)\cos^2 x$. The substitution $u=\cos x$ runs from 1 to 0 and the stored value is $2/15$. A leftover factor of $\sin x$ or $\cos x$ means a substitution, not a reduction.

\section{A second family}
For a positive integer $n$,
\[
J_n=\int_0^\infty x^n e^{-x}\,dx.
\]
Parts with $u=x^n$ gives $J_n=n J_{n-1}$, and $J_0=1$, so $J_n=n!$. In particular $J_3=6$. This is not one of the eight drills. It is the same index drop, and it is the integer case of the Gamma integral in unit 14. Stop when the index hits 0.

\section{An alternating family}
Guide section 1.3 also reduces a rational power by algebra, not only by parts. For an integer $n\ge 0$,
\[
I_n=\int_0^1\frac{x^n}{1+x}\,dx.
\]
Adding the neighbour cancels the denominator:
\[
I_n+I_{n-1}=\int_0^1 x^{n-1}\,dx=\frac1n,\qquad n\ge 1.
\]
The base is the add-and-subtract move of \texttt{docs/techniques/introduce-constant.md}:
\[
I_0=\int_0^1\frac{1}{1+x}\,dx=\ln 2,
\qquad
I_1=\int_0^1\frac{x}{1+x}\,dx=1-\ln 2.
\]
Then $I_2=1/2-I_1=\ln 2-1/2$ and $I_3=1/3-I_2=5/6-\ln 2$. This family is not one of the eight drills. Do not mark $I_3$ as \texttt{U20-D01}. The index still drops by a fixed integer, and the step changes sign. A leftover factor of $\sin x$ is still a substitution. A continuous parameter is still unit 34.

\section{Faded step --- the rational base}
Cover the values above. For $I_2$, write $I_2=1/2-I_1$ and substitute the base $1-\ln 2$. The result is $\ln 2-1/2$. If the integrand had been $\sin^2 x\cos^2 x$, you would be in the faded step for \texttt{U20-D02} instead, and you would not open this family.
