\section{Learning goals}
Turn a rational function of sine and cosine into a rational function of $t$.
Guide section 1.6. The substitution is $t=\tan(x/2)$.

\section{The three replacements}
From the double-angle formulas,
\[
\sin x=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\qquad
\cos x=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\qquad
dx=\frac{2\,dt}{1+t^2}.
\]
Use this when the integrand is rational in $\sin x$ and $\cos x$.
A plain sine power is a reduction, not this substitution.

\section{Worked --- \texttt{U26-D01}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{1}{1+\sin x}\,dx.
\]
The limits $x=0$ and $x=\pi/2$ become $t=0$ and $t=1$.
\[
1+\sin x=\frac{(1+t)^2}{1+t^2},
\]
so the integrand collapses to $2/(1+t)^2$.
\[
\int_0^1\frac{2}{(1+t)^2}\,dt=\Bigl[\frac{-2}{1+t}\Bigr]_0^1=1.
\]
That is the stored answer.

\section{Faded step --- \texttt{U26-D02}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi}\frac{1}{2+\cos x}\,dx.
\]
Put $t=\tan(x/2)$. The stored answer is $\pi/\sqrt{3}$.
Check the endpoints before you expand the algebra. At $x=\pi$ the half-angle tangent is unbounded, so split at $\pi/2$ or quote the standard definite form after the substitution is in place.
