\section{Learning goals}
Turn a rational function of sine and cosine into a rational function of $t$.
The substitution is $t=\tan(x/2)$. Check the endpoints before you expand. A plain sine power is a reduction, unit 20, not this substitution. A rational function of $\tan x$ with a $\sec^2 x$ already present is $t=\tan x$, which is unit 09's ordinary substitution.

\section{The three replacements}
The half-angle formulas give
\[
\sin x=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\qquad
\cos x=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\qquad
dx=\frac{2\,dt}{1+t^2},
\]
where $t=\tan(x/2)$. They are the double-angle formulas written in $t$, together with $x=2\arctan t$. Use them when the integrand is rational in $\sin x$ and $\cos x$.

\section{Worked --- \texttt{U26-D01}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{1}{1+\sin x}\,dx.
\]
The limits $x=0$ and $x=\pi/2$ become $t=0$ and $t=1$.
\[
1+\sin x=\frac{(1+t)^2}{1+t^2},
\]
so the integrand collapses to $2/(1+t)^2$.
\[
\int_0^1\frac{2}{(1+t)^2}\,dt=\Bigl[\frac{-2}{1+t}\Bigr]_0^1=1.
\]
That is the stored answer.

The indefinite integral of the same integrand is stored as \texttt{U26-D04}, $\tan x-\sec x$. The half-angle antiderivative $-2/(1+\tan(x/2))$ equals that expression plus 1 on $(0,\pi)$. Either form is valid. The drill stores $\tan x-\sec x$.

\section{A definite pattern --- \texttt{U26-D02}}
For $a>|b|$,
\[
\int_0^{\pi}\frac{dx}{a+b\cos x}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}.
\]
The derivation is the same substitution. As $x$ runs over $[0,\pi)$, $t=\tan(x/2)$ runs over $[0,\infty)$, and
\[
a+b\cos x=\frac{(a-b)t^2+(a+b)}{1+t^2}.
\]
The $1+t^2$ cancels the factor in $dx$, and
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{2\,dt}{(a-b)t^2+(a+b)}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}.
\]
\texttt{U26-D02} is $a=2$, $b=1$, so the value is $\pi/\sqrt{3}$. \texttt{U26-D03} is $a=3$, $b=2$, value $\pi/\sqrt{5}$. \texttt{U26-D05} is $a=5$, $b=4$, value $\pi/3$. The hypothesis $a>|b|$ keeps the denominator from vanishing. At $x=\pi$ the half-angle tangent is unbounded, so the definite integral is the improper integral in $t$, which converges.

\section{Both sine and cosine --- \texttt{U26-D06}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{1+\sin x+\cos x}.
\]
Here $t$ runs from 0 to 1. The numerator $2\,dt/(1+t^2)$ cancels against
\[
1+\sin x+\cos x=\frac{2(1+t)}{1+t^2},
\]
and the integral is $\int_0^1 dt/(1+t)=\ln 2$. That is the stored answer.

\section{Faded step --- \texttt{U26-D08}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{2+\sin x}.
\]
Put $t=\tan(x/2)$ again. The limits are $0$ and $1$, not $0$ and $\infty$. The stored answer is $\sqrt{3}\,\pi/9$. \texttt{U26-D07} shortens the upper limit of \texttt{U26-D02} to $\pi/3$. Do not quote the full $[0,\pi]$ formula when the interval is shorter.

\section{What this is not}
A reduction $I_n=((n-1)/n)I_{n-2}$ is unit 20. Adding a reflected copy is unit 10. Introducing a constant in a rational function of $x$ is \texttt{docs/techniques/introduce-constant.md}. If the integrand is not rational in sine and cosine, this substitution makes a harder integral.
