Two moves carry this unit. \texttt{ninja-substitution} tries $x=\tan\theta$ when $1+x^2$ is in the denominator, and it tries a radical that collapses to an arctangent. \texttt{inversion} is $x=1/t$ on $(0,\infty)$, the half-line case of unit 10. Yang \S\S6.4--6.5 names them. The substitutions themselves are ordinary. The route is \texttt{docs/techniques/concepts/habits.md}.

\section{Learning goals}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Try $x=\tan\theta$ when you see $1+x^2$ in the denominator and a logarithm or a trig function of $x$ in the numerator.
  \item On $(0,\infty)$, write the integral at $1/x$ and add the two copies.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked tangent substitution --- \texttt{U33-D04}}
\[
I=\int_0^1\frac{\ln(1+x)}{1+x^2}\,dx.
\]
Put $x=\tan\theta$, so $\theta$ runs from $0$ to $\pi/4$ and $dx/(1+x^2)=d\theta$. Then $I=\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(1+\tan\theta)\,d\theta$. The reflection $\theta\mapsto\pi/4-\theta$ uses
\[
1+\tan(\pi/4-\theta)=\frac{2}{1+\tan\theta},
\]
so $\ln(1+\tan(\pi/4-\theta))=\ln 2-\ln(1+\tan\theta)$. Adding the two copies gives $2I=(\pi/4)\ln 2$, hence $I=\pi\ln 2/8$. That is the stored answer.

\texttt{U33-D01} is the plain substitution $\int_0^1 x/(1+x^2)\,dx=(\ln 2)/2$. The numerator is already the derivative of the inside. A tangent substitution is the long way around.

A scale of the tangent differential, not a drill, drops the logarithm:
\[
\int_0^1\frac{\arctan x}{1+x^2}\,dx.
\]
Set $u=\arctan x$. Then $u$ runs from $0$ to $\pi/4$ and the integral is $\int_0^{\pi/4}u\,du=\pi^2/32$. SymPy checks $\pi^2/32$.

\section{A rational trig integral --- \texttt{U33-D02}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{1+\sin^2 x}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}.
\]
Write $\sin^2 x=(1-\cos 2x)/2$, so $1+\sin^2 x=(3-\cos 2x)/2$. The integral becomes $\int_0^{\pi} du/(3-\cos u)$ after $u=2x$. Unit 26 evaluates $\int_0^{\pi} du/(a+b\cos u)=\pi/\sqrt{a^2-b^2}$ for $a>|b|$. Here $a=3$ and $b=-1$, and $\sqrt{9-1}=2\sqrt{2}$. The drill feed prints the rationalized form $\sqrt{2}\,\pi/4$. A 30-digit evaluation of the original integral matches that number.

\section{Worked inversion --- \texttt{U33-D06} (\texttt{inversion})}
\[
I=\int_0^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(1+x^2)(1+x)}.
\]
The substitution $x=1/t$ produces $\int_0^{\infty} t\,dt/((1+t^2)(1+t))$. Add the two copies. The numerators $1$ and $x$ cancel $1+x$:
\[
2I=\int_0^{\infty}\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\frac\pi2,
\]
so $I=\pi/4$. That is the stored value.

\texttt{U33-D05} is $\int_0^{\infty} dx/(1+x^2)=\pi/2$. Inversion reproduces the same integral. Stop at the arctangent.

\section{The logarithm changes sign --- \texttt{U33-D07}}
\[
I=\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx.
\]
Set $x=1/t$. Then $\ln x=-\ln t$ and $dx/(1+x^2)=-dt/(1+t^2)$. The product of those two minuses is positive, so the integrand in $t$ is $\ln t/(1+t^2)$. The endpoint $x=0^+$ maps to $t=\infty$ and $x=\infty$ maps to $t=0^+$:
\[
I=\int_{\infty}^{0}\frac{\ln t}{1+t^2}\,dt=-I.
\]
Hence $I=0$, the stored value. The same sign flip is unit 10.

\section{Faded, skip the inversion --- \texttt{U33-D08}}
\[
\int_0^{\infty}\frac{x}{(1+x^2)^2}\,dx.
\]
The numerator is half the derivative of the inside. Set $u=1+x^2$, so $du=2x\,dx$ and $u$ runs from $1$ to $\infty$:
\[
\frac12\int_1^{\infty}u^{-2}\,du=\frac12.
\]
That is the stored value. Inversion is a longer path. The same substitution on the finite interval $[0,1]$ is a scale, not a drill:
\[
\int_0^1\frac{x}{(1+x^2)^2}\,dx=\frac14.
\]
SymPy checks $1/4$.

\section{Faded radical --- \texttt{U33-D03}}
\[
\int\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}}.
\]
Set $u=\sqrt{x^2-1}$, so $x^2=u^2+1$ and $2x\,dx=2u\,du$. Then $dx/x=u\,du/x^2=u\,du/(u^2+1)$, and dividing by the remaining $u$ leaves
\[
\int\frac{du}{u^2+1}=\arctan u=\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}.
\]
That is the stored antiderivative, on $x>1$. Differentiating the stored answer recovers the integrand. The blank is the substitution $u$.
