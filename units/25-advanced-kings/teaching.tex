Unit 10 is the reflection toolkit. This unit is the same substitution $u=a+b-x$ when the integrand is an exponential, a tangent, or a factor of $x$ on $[0,\pi]$. Yang \S4.4 is a later list of those shapes. There is still one identity.

\section{Learning goals}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Reflect, add the two copies, and only then integrate.
  \item If the reflected integrand is not a complement, keep both copies. The sum may still simplify.
  \item Do not quote $\pi/4$ for a tangent integral whose sum is not constant.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked exponential --- \texttt{U25-D03}}
\[
I=\int_0^1\frac{e^x}{e^x+e^{1-x}}\,dx.
\]
The map $x\mapsto 1-x$ swaps $e^x$ with $e^{1-x}$. The reflected integrand is the other fraction, and the two fractions add to 1. So $2I=\int_0^1 1\,dx=1$ and $I=1/2$.

\section{The sine-cosine case, one step harder --- \texttt{U25-D05}}
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^3 x}{\sin^3 x+\cos^3 x}\,dx=\frac\pi4.
\]
The swap $x\mapsto\pi/2-x$ exchanges the cubes. The sum of the two integrands is 1, the interval has length $\pi/2$, and half of that length is $\pi/4$. \texttt{U25-D01} and \texttt{U25-D02} are the first-power versions from unit 10. \texttt{U25-D08} does not need a reflection: $u=\sin x$ gives $1/2$.

\section{Worked tangent --- \texttt{U25-D04}}
\[
I=\int_0^{\pi/4}\frac{dx}{1+\tan x}.
\]
The map $x\mapsto\pi/4-x$ uses $\tan(\pi/4-x)=(1-\tan x)/(1+\tan x)$, so
\[
\frac{1}{1+\tan(\pi/4-x)}=\frac{1+\tan x}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
I=\int_0^{\pi/4}\frac{1+\tan x}{2}\,dx=\frac\pi8+\frac12\int_0^{\pi/4}\tan x\,dx=\frac\pi8+\frac14\ln 2.
\]
The stored answer is $\pi/8+(\ln 2)/4$. Reflection did not make the integrand constant. Adding the copies still removed the hard fraction.

\section{A factor of $x$ --- \texttt{U25-D06}}
\[
I=\int_0^{\pi}\frac{x}{1+\sin x}\,dx.
\]
The map $x\mapsto\pi-x$ leaves $\sin x$ fixed and sends $x$ to $\pi-x$, so
\[
2I=\pi\int_0^{\pi}\frac{dx}{1+\sin x}.
\]
The half-angle substitution of unit 26, with a split because $t=\tan(x/2)$ runs to infinity as $x$ approaches $\pi$, gives $\int_0^{\pi} dx/(1+\sin x)=2$. Hence $I=\pi$. That is the stored answer. \texttt{U25-D07} is the unit 10 warmup $x/(1+x)$ on $[0,1]$, value $1-\ln 2$, by writing the integrand as $1-1/(1+x)$.
