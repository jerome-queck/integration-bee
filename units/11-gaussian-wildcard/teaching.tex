\section{Learning goals}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Evaluate an elementary Gaussian cousin, and quote the full Gaussian when it is the integrand.
  \item Split a floor or a fractional part at the integers and add the pieces.
  \item Differentiate or integrate a parameter on a fixed interval. Do not build a parameter when a substitution already finishes the integral.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked Gaussian --- \texttt{U11-D01}}
\[
\int_0^1 x e^{-x^2}\,dx=\frac{1-e^{-1}}{2}.
\]
Let $u=x^2$. \texttt{U11-D02} is the same integrand on $[-1,1]$ and is odd, so the value is 0. \texttt{U11-D03} is the full Gaussian, $\sqrt\pi$. \texttt{U11-D04} is $\int_0^{\infty}e^{-x}\,dx=1$. \texttt{U11-D08} is $\int_0^{\infty}x e^{-x}\,dx=1$, which is $J_1$ from unit 14.

\section{Wildcard openers}
\texttt{U11-D05} is $\int_{-1}^{1}dx/(1+x^2)=\pi/2$. \texttt{U11-D06} is even, so split $|x|e^{-|x|}$ at 0. \texttt{U11-D07} is the reflection $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x/(\sin x+\cos x)\,dx=\pi/4$, the same move as unit 10.

\section{Worked floor --- \texttt{U11-D09}}
On each half-open interval $[k,k+1)$ the floor is the constant $k$. You split at integers, pull that constant out, and add the areas. A single endpoint does not change the value.
\[
\int_0^4\lfloor x\rfloor\,dx=0\cdot 1+1\cdot 1+2\cdot 1+3\cdot 1=6.
\]
That is the stored answer. The machine feed stores the piecewise split, because a direct call to \texttt{floor} stays unevaluated. The number is the floor integral.

\section{Faded floor}
\texttt{U11-D11} stops at $5/2$, not at an integer. The last piece has length $1/2$ and height $2$, so it contributes $1$, and the total is $2$. Do not quote $n(n-1)/2$.

\texttt{U11-D10} is the fractional part $\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor$ on $[0,3]$. Each tooth integrates to $1/2$, so three teeth give $3/2$.

\texttt{U11-D12} is a negative argument. On $[-2,-1)$ the floor is $-2$, and on $[-1,0)$ it is $-1$. The value is $-3$. Do not replace the floor by truncation toward zero.

The worked fences, including a non-constant factor left inside a piece, stay in \texttt{docs/techniques/floor.md}. An infinite upper limit becomes a series. Do not quote a zeta value.

\section{Floor dose}
Worked reads \texttt{U11-D09} and draws its four steps. Faded sets \texttt{U11-D11} with only the split at $1$ and $2$; ask for the last piece's length before uncovering the total. Bare sets \texttt{U11-D10} without the sawtooth hint. A negative-argument miss sets \texttt{U11-D12}, after asking whether floor means rounding down or truncating toward zero. The same drill ids still serve the Round 1 \texttt{special-wildcard} route.

\section{Worked parameter --- \texttt{U11-D13}}
For $a>-1$,
\[
\int_0^1 x^a\,dx=\frac{1}{a+1}.
\]
On $[\varepsilon,1]$ the derivative in $a$ passes under the integral sign. To let $\varepsilon\to 0$, restrict $a$ to a compact interval $[\alpha,\beta]$ with $\alpha>-1$. The derivative is bounded in absolute value by $x^\alpha|\ln x|$, an integrable function on $(0,1)$. This justifies the limit and gives $\int_0^1x^a\ln x\,dx=-1/(a+1)^2$. At $a=4$ the value is $-1/25$, the stored answer of \texttt{U11-D13}. The fence at $a=3$ is in \texttt{docs/techniques/introduce-constant.md}. \texttt{U34-D05} is the same family at $a=2$.

\section{Two more fixed intervals}
\texttt{U11-D14} starts from $\int_0^{\infty}e^{-ax}\,dx=1/a$ for $a>0$. Differentiate in $a$ and set $a=2$:
\[
\int_0^{\infty}x e^{-2x}\,dx=\frac14.
\]
Take a compact upper limit first. For parameters $a\ge c>0$, the derivative is bounded in absolute value by $xe^{-cx}$, which is integrable. That bound justifies letting the upper limit tend to infinity after differentiating.

\texttt{U11-D15} integrates the parameter instead of differentiating it. Integrate $1/(a+1)$ for $a$ from 0 to 3:
\[
\int_0^1\frac{x^3-1}{\ln x}\,dx=\ln 4.
\]
A moving endpoint is a different syllabus formula. It is unit 34, and it is not one of these three drills.
