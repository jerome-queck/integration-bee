\section{Learning goals}
Differentiate a parameter when the integrand is a derivative in that parameter.
Know when a plain substitution already finishes the integral, so you do not build a parameter you do not need.

\section{The rule you are using}
If $f$ and $\partial f/\partial a$ are continuous for $x$ in a compact interval $[c,d]$ and $a$ in a compact interval of parameters, then
\[
\frac{d}{da}\int_c^d f(x,a)\,dx = \int_c^d \partial_a f(x,a)\,dx.
\]
An endpoint that blows up, or an infinite limit, is a limit of compact pieces. Take that limit after the derivative, or use a positive integrand so the integrals may be switched.

\section{A moving limit}
The syllabus writes a second form. If the endpoints move with the parameter and $f$ is continuously differentiable in the parameter on the region you use,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)} f(x,t)\,dx
=
\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)}\partial_t f(x,t)\,dx
+ f(b(t),t)\,b'(t)
- f(a(t),t)\,a'(t).
\]
The two new terms are the fundamental theorem. They are the whole answer when $f$ does not depend on $t$.

This integral is not one of the eight drills. Let $f(x,t)=tx$, $a(t)=0$, and $b(t)=t$. Then
\[
I(t)=\int_0^t tx\,dx=\frac{t^3}{2},\qquad I'(t)=\frac{3t^2}{2}.
\]
At $t=2$ the integral equals 4 and the derivative equals 6. The formula at that point is $\int_0^2 x\,dx=2$ from the parameter, plus the upper boundary term $f(2,2)\cdot 1=4$, and the lower boundary is 0. The sum is 6. Adding and subtracting a constant in the integrand is \texttt{docs/techniques/introduce-constant.md}. This derivative is not that algebra. Do not tick \texttt{dutis}.

\section{Faded moving limit}
Cover the example. If $f$ does not depend on $t$ and the upper limit is $t$, the derivative is $f(t)$. For $\int_0^t\sin x\,dx$, that derivative is $\sin t$. If you only needed this sentence, do not introduce a parameter inside the integrand.

\section{Worked parameter --- \texttt{U34-D05}}
For $a>-1$,
\[
\int_0^1 x^a\,dx=\frac{1}{a+1}.
\]
On $[\varepsilon,1]$ the Leibniz rule applies. Let $\varepsilon\to 0$. Differentiate in $a$:
\[
\int_0^1 x^a\ln x\,dx=-\frac{1}{(a+1)^2}.
\]
At $a=2$ the value is $-1/9$, the stored answer of \texttt{U34-D05}.
The drill file stores $x^2\log x$ on $[0,1]$. It does not store the parameter $a$. The note \texttt{docs/techniques/introduce-constant.md} runs the same family at $a=3$.

\section{Integrate the parameter --- \texttt{U34-D04}}
Integrate the identity $\int_0^1 x^a\,dx=1/(a+1)$ for $a$ from 0 to 2:
\[
\int_0^1\frac{x^2-1}{\ln x}\,dx=\ln 3.
\]
The left side is the stored drill. \texttt{U36-D07} is the same integral, and \texttt{U36-D01} is the same argument from $a=0$ to $a=1$, stored value $\ln 2$. One parameter, three drills.

\section{Frullani by switching integrals --- \texttt{U34-D01}}
For $b>a>0$ and $x>0$,
\[
\frac{e^{-ax}-e^{-bx}}{x}=\int_a^b e^{-xt}\,dt.
\]
The integrand is positive. Integrate in $x$ from 0 to $\infty$ and switch the order:
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-ax}-e^{-bx}}{x}\,dx=\int_a^b\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}\,dx\,dt=\int_a^b\frac{dt}{t}=\ln\frac{b}{a}.
\]
\texttt{U34-D01} is $a=1$, $b=2$, stored answer $\ln 2$. \texttt{U34-D02} is $b=3$, stored answer $\ln 3$. \texttt{U35-D07} is the same integral as \texttt{U34-D01}.

\texttt{U34-D03} replaces the exponentials by cosines, $( \cos x-\cos 2x)/x$ on $[0,\infty)$, stored answer $\ln 2$. The same parameter count produces it. The printed sheet may still show letters. The SymPy row is the concrete pair.

\section{A substitution wearing a parameter's clothes}
\texttt{U34-D07} is $\int_0^1 \ln(1+x)/(1+x)\,dx$. Let $u=\ln(1+x)$. Then $du=dx/(1+x)$ and the limits run from 0 to $\ln 2$, so the value is $(\ln 2)^2/2$. No parameter is required. \texttt{U34-D08} is $\int_0^\infty x e^{-x}\,dx=1$, which is $J_1$ from unit 20. \texttt{U34-D06} is $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\sin x\,dx=1/2$, also the real or imaginary part of a complex exponential (unit 32).
