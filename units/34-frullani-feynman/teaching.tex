\section{Learning goals}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Differentiate a parameter when the integrand is a derivative in that parameter.
  \item Include both boundary terms when an endpoint moves, and justify differentiation of an improper integral.
  \item Know when a plain substitution or one parts step already finishes the integral, so you do not build a parameter you do not need.
  \item Refuse $\pi/2$ on a difference of cosines. That value is the Dirichlet integral, not Frullani.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The rule you are using}
If $f$ and $\partial f/\partial a$ are continuous for $x$ in a compact interval $[c,d]$ and $a$ in a compact interval of parameters, then
\[
\frac{d}{da}\int_c^d f(x,a)\,dx = \int_c^d \partial_a f(x,a)\,dx.
\]
An endpoint that blows up, or an infinite limit, needs an extra argument: find an integrable bound for $|\partial_a f|$ that works throughout a neighborhood of the parameter before passing the derivative through the improper limit. Nonnegative integrands justify switching two integrals; positivity alone does not justify differentiating an improper integral.

\section{A moving limit}
The syllabus writes a second form. If the endpoints $a(t)$ and $b(t)$ are differentiable, and $f$ and $\partial_t f$ are continuous on the region you use,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)} f(x,t)\,dx
=
\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)}\partial_t f(x,t)\,dx
+ f(b(t),t)\,b'(t)
- f(a(t),t)\,a'(t).
\]
The two new terms are the fundamental theorem. They are the whole answer when $f$ does not depend on $t$.

This integral is not one of the eight drills. Let $f(x,t)=tx$, $a(t)=0$, and $b(t)=t$. Then
\[
I(t)=\int_0^t tx\,dx=\frac{t^3}{2},\qquad I'(t)=\frac{3t^2}{2}.
\]
At $t=2$ the integral equals 4 and the derivative equals 6. The formula at that point is $\int_0^2 x\,dx=2$ from the parameter, plus the upper boundary term $f(2,2)\cdot 1=4$, and the lower boundary is 0. The sum is 6. Adding and subtracting a constant in the integrand is \texttt{docs/techniques/introduce-constant.md}. This derivative is not that algebra.

If $f$ does not depend on $t$ and the upper limit is $t$, the derivative is $f(t)$. For $\int_0^t\sin x\,dx$, that derivative is $\sin t$. That sentence is not a drill. If you only needed it, do not introduce a parameter inside the integrand.

\section{Worked Frullani --- \texttt{U34-D01}}
For $b>a>0$ and $x>0$,
\[
\frac{e^{-ax}-e^{-bx}}{x}=\int_a^b e^{-xt}\,dt.
\]
The integrand is positive. Integrate in $x$ from 0 to $\infty$ and switch the order:
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-ax}-e^{-bx}}{x}\,dx=\int_a^b\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}\,dx\,dt=\int_a^b\frac{dt}{t}=\ln\frac{b}{a}.
\]
\texttt{U34-D01} is $a=1$, $b=2$, stored answer $\ln 2$. The worked dose stops there. \texttt{U35-D07} is the same integral.

\section{Faded --- \texttt{U34-D02}}
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-x}-e^{-3x}}{x}\,dx.
\]
The blank is the ratio $b/a$. Here $a=1$ and $b=3$. After that ratio is written, uncover $\ln 3$. Refuse $\ln 2$, the stored answer of \texttt{U34-D01}.

\section{Bare --- \texttt{U34-D03}}
Set
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos x-\cos 2x}{x}\,dx
\]
and show nothing else. The bare dose hides the parameter. After a line is written, uncover it. The same count as \texttt{U34-D01} produces $\ln 2$. The printed sheet may still show letters. The SymPy row is the concrete pair. Refuse $\pi/2$.

\section{Worked parameter --- \texttt{U34-D05}}
For $a>-1$,
\[
\int_0^1 x^a\,dx=\frac{1}{a+1}.
\]
On $[\varepsilon,1]$ the Leibniz rule applies. For $a$ in a compact interval $[\alpha,\beta]$ with $\alpha>-1$, $|x^a\ln x|\le x^\alpha|\ln x|$ on $(0,1)$. This integrable bound justifies letting $\varepsilon\to0$ after differentiating in $a$:
\[
\int_0^1 x^a\ln x\,dx=-\frac{1}{(a+1)^2}.
\]
At $a=2$ the value is $-1/9$, the stored answer of \texttt{U34-D05}. The worked dose stops there.
The drill file stores $x^2\log x$ on $[0,1]$. It does not store the parameter $a$. The note \texttt{docs/techniques/introduce-constant.md} runs the same family at $a=3$.

\section{Faded --- \texttt{U34-D04}}
\[
\int_0^1\frac{x^2-1}{\ln x}\,dx.
\]
The blank is the interval of $a$. Integrate $\int_0^1 x^a\,dx=1/(a+1)$ for $a$ from 0 to 2. After that interval is written, uncover $\ln 3$. Refuse $-1/9$, the stored answer of \texttt{U34-D05}. \texttt{U36-D07} is this integral. \texttt{U36-D01} is the same argument from $a=0$ to $a=1$, stored value $\ln 2$.

\section{Bare --- \texttt{U34-D08}}
Set
\[
\int_0^\infty x e^{-x}\,dx
\]
and show nothing else. The bare dose hides the decision to skip the parameter. After a line is written, uncover $J_1$ from unit 20. The stored answer is $1$.

\section{Refusal --- a parameter you do not need, or $\pi/2$}
\texttt{U34-D07} is $\int_0^1 \ln(1+x)/(1+x)\,dx$. Let $u=\ln(1+x)$. Then $du=dx/(1+x)$ and the limits run from 0 to $\ln 2$, so the value is $(\ln 2)^2/2$. Refuse a parameter. That is the stored answer.

\texttt{U34-D06} is $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\sin x\,dx$. The stored answer is $1/2$. One parts step finishes it, or the imaginary part of a complex exponential. The same number is \texttt{U32-D06} and \texttt{U30-D07}. Refuse a parameter, and refuse $\pi/2$.

\texttt{U34-D03} stores $\ln 2$. The Dirichlet value $\pi/2$ is \texttt{U30-D05}, a different integrand. Refuse it here.

\section{Dose}
For \texttt{frullani}, worked reads \texttt{U34-D01}, then the faded ratio on \texttt{U34-D02}, then sets \texttt{U34-D03}. Faded gives only that ratio. Bare sets \texttt{U34-D03}. The value $\pi/2$ opens the refusal and sets \texttt{U34-D03}.

For \texttt{feynman}, worked reads \texttt{U34-D05}. The moving-limit case, derivative $6$ at $t=2$, stays in this file and is not a drill. Then the faded interval on \texttt{U34-D04}, then sets \texttt{U34-D08}. Faded gives only that interval of $a$. Bare sets \texttt{U34-D08}. A parameter built for a substitution opens the refusal and sets \texttt{U34-D07}. A parameter built for $e^{-x}\sin x$ opens the refusal and sets \texttt{U34-D06}.

For \texttt{dutis}, use the same stored worked and faded parameter drills, \texttt{U34-D05} and \texttt{U34-D04}. Before the bare drill, give an oral recognition check on $I(t)=\int_0^t tx\,dx$: ask for the interior derivative and both boundary terms without showing their sum. The answer at $t=2$ is $2+4-0=6$, already derived above. This check distinguishes moving endpoints from a fixed interval; it has no machine-drill id and awards no automatic credit. Bare then sets \texttt{U11-D14}, with the request to justify a parameter derivative; the stored answer is $1/4$. The other introductory fixed-interval alternatives are \texttt{U11-D13} and \texttt{U11-D15}. The refusal drills above remain useful comparisons, but they do not count as direct \texttt{dutis} evidence.
