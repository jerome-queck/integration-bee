The integral of $\sin(mx)\sin(nx)$ over a full number of periods is classical Fourier arithmetic. Yang \S\S3.7--3.8 is the contest packaging: read the square before you expand, and split a sum the moment you see it.

\section{Learning goals}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item On $[0,2\pi]$, for positive integers $m\neq n$, the products $\sin(mx)\sin(nx)$ and $\cos(mx)\cos(nx)$ integrate to 0. The square $\sin^2(mx)$ integrates to $\pi$.
  \item A square of a sum keeps the diagonal. Cross terms die.
  \item Speed means split, then integrate. It does not mean a new identity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked orthogonality --- \texttt{U23-D01}}
\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\sin(3x)\sin(5x)\,dx=0,
\]
because $3\neq 5$. The product-to-sum formula gives the same zero in one line. \texttt{U23-D05} is the cosine partner, also 0. \texttt{U23-D03} is $\sin(23x)\sin(19x)$ on $[0,\pi]$, still 0 for distinct positive integers.

\texttt{U23-D04} is the diagonal: $\int_0^{2\pi}20\sin^2 x\,dx=20\pi$.

\section{Watch the square --- \texttt{U23-D02}}
\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\bigl(\sin x+\sin 2x+\sin 3x\bigr)^2\,dx.
\]
Expand. Three cross terms integrate to 0. Three squares remain, and each $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2(mx)\,dx$ equals $\pi$. The value is $3\pi$. If you drop the squares and keep only the cross terms, you will report 0.

\section{Speed is a split --- \texttt{U23-D08} (\texttt{speed-bashing})}
\[
\int\Bigl(\frac{1}{x^2}+\frac{1}{x}+1-\frac{1}{x^2+1}\Bigr)dx.
\]
Each term has a textbook antiderivative. Add them:
\[
x+\ln|x|-\arctan x-\frac{1}{x}+C.
\]
That is the stored answer. A shorter sum, scaled from this split and not a drill, is
\[
\int_0^1\Bigl(x+\frac{1}{1+x}\Bigr)dx=\Bigl[\frac{x^2}{2}+\ln(1+x)\Bigr]_0^1=\frac12+\ln 2.
\]
SymPy checks $\frac12+\ln 2$.

\section{Three parts steps --- \texttt{U23-D06}}
\[
\int x^3 e^x\,dx.
\]
Parts with $u=x^3$ and $dv=e^x\,dx$, three times:
\begin{align*}
\int x^3 e^x\,dx
&= x^3 e^x-3\int x^2 e^x\,dx\\
&= x^3 e^x-3x^2 e^x+6\int x e^x\,dx\\
&= e^x(x^3-3x^2+6x-6)+C.
\end{align*}
That is the stored antiderivative. The same shape with a lower degree is unit 03 and unit 28. Here the speed is to unroll the three steps and stop.

\section{Faded odd powers --- \texttt{U23-D07}}
\[
\int(\sin^3 x+\cos^3 x)\,dx.
\]
Split the sum. Write $\sin^3 x=\sin x(1-\cos^2 x)$ and set $u=\cos x$:
\[
\int\sin^3 x\,dx=-\cos x+\frac{\cos^3 x}{3}.
\]
Write $\cos^3 x=\cos x(1-\sin^2 x)$ and set $v=\sin x$:
\[
\int\cos^3 x\,dx=\sin x-\frac{\sin^3 x}{3}.
\]
The stored antiderivative is the sum
\[
-\cos x+\frac{\cos^3 x}{3}+\sin x-\frac{\sin^3 x}{3}+C.
\]
The power is 3, and a factor of $\sin x$ or $\cos x$ is already the derivative of the other function. A reduction $I_n$ waits for a higher even power. That reduction is unit 20.
