# Contest formats

Round 2 and Round 3 are games. A mark is still one final answer. The answer is often a digit, a short fill, or the chosen part of a pair. These notes cite the 2024 participant guides and do not copy a contest answer. The named formats are group, crossnumber, relay, chainlink, dichotomy, and tournament.

The Yang Topics Guide does not teach these formats. The sources are the participant guides and the contest site https://integration.soc.srcf.net/. Practice the answer-shape of the format with unit drills. The integral skill is still the unit note.

## What the mark is

Write the object the blank asks for. A group blank wants the value. A crossnumber square wants one digit. A relay wants the current problem only. If the written answer is a full antiderivative and the blank wanted a digit, the miss is the format, and this file is the note to teach. Do not paste a contest solution into the note.

## Group

Ten problems, about an hour, one team. A correct answer scores. A blank or a wrong answer scores nothing. The problems rise in difficulty. This is the closest format to a Round 1 paper.

Train by working a timed set of ten unit drills, hardest last. Skip a stuck integral instead of spending the hour on it. There is no partial credit for a method.

## Crossnumber

A grid of digits. One half of the team has the across clues and the other half has the down clues. They may ask each other to attempt a clue. They may not say the answer. A grader writes the correct digit into a master grid after a square is attempted, and that square cannot be attempted again. One attempt per square. The 2024 blank grid file is named Crossword.

Train by splitting a page: one person sees only the across clues, the other only the down clues. Agree a digit only by pointing at a clue, not by saying the value. After a wrong digit, that square is gone, so check the units digit before you commit.

## Relay

Problems arrive one at a time. The team solves or skips the current problem before seeing the next one, and it cannot return. The 2021 file is named Shuttle. Later years name the same format Relay. A run of correct answers can add a bonus on top of the per-problem score.

Train by covering the next drill until the current one is answered or skipped. A skip is allowed. Going back is not. The bonus rewards a streak, so a confident early answer is worth more than a long fight on problem one.

## Chainlink

Five sets of eight problems. They arrive one at a time, in rising difficulty, as in the relay. After a correct answer the team banks the chain or continues. A failed problem ends the game at the highest safety net already reached. Lifelines can void a problem or add a safety net. The second half revisits unused problems, and a skip there breaks the chain.

Train the decision, not only the integral. After each correct drill, say out loud whether you would bank. Banking keeps the safety net. Continuing risks it for a longer chain. In the second half, treat a skip as the end of the chain.

## Dichotomy

Each segment is pairs of problems. The selectors see both problems in a pair and choose one. The solver does not see the other one, and the two roles do not talk. The solver receives problems one at a time and cannot return. A first-attempt answer scores more than a later attempt. Selectors and the solver rotate across segments.

Train with two drills face down. The selector looks at both integrands, picks the one the solver is more likely to finish, and passes only that one. The solver does not ask what the other one was. Score a clean first answer above a corrected one.

## Tournament

Teams meet on the same problems in a bracket. Each battle is three fights, and each fight is three problems on a board. Speed can award a point even when both teams solve. A contestant may declare a double-or-nothing attempt before a fight. An eliminated team can still submit a redemption answer on paper before the boards finish. The top team from the earlier rounds enters with a reserved path into a later battle.

Train by racing a partner on three drills. Call the double before you see the integrals, and only when you want the swing. A wrong double costs the fight. Redemption is a written answer after elimination, so keep a clean final line even when the board has moved on.

## On a miss

Teach this file. Name the format, the object of the answer, and the rule the attempt broke (a second try at a square, a return to an earlier relay problem, a spoken digit, a double called too late). Then set an ordinary unit drill. Do not invent a contest answer.
